Fitness and health calculators

Four adult formulas: body mass index, a calorie estimate, a circumference body-fat estimate, and running pace. None of them is a diagnosis.

BMI is weight divided by height squared. Kilograms and centimetres are converted with the exact factors 0.45359237 kilograms per pound and 0.0254 metres per inch, so the same body in either unit system returns the same index. The categories are the adult World Health Organization cut points: under 18.5, 18.5 to 24.9, 25 to 29.9, and 30 or more. They are not cut points for children, athletes, or pregnancy.

The calorie page uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation. The male form adds 5 and the female form subtracts 161, after 10 times weight in kilograms, 6.25 times height in centimetres, and minus 5 times age. An activity factor from 1.2 to 1.9 turns that basal figure into an estimate of daily calories. Five hundred kilocalories below or above that line is the common half-kilogram-a-week sketch. It is not a prescription, and it is not a measurement of your metabolism.

Body fat uses the US Navy circumference equation in inches, with centimetres converted by 2.54 before the base-10 logarithm. Men use abdomen minus neck. Women use waist plus hip minus neck. Pace divides time by distance and also reports kilometres per hour. A 10 kilometre run in 50 minutes is 5 minutes per kilometre and 12 kilometres per hour. The page does not read a watch or a heart-rate strap.

Questions people ask

Is BMI a measure of health?

It is weight divided by height squared, sorted with adult WHO cut points. It does not see muscle, bone, age, or a medical history.

Where do the calorie numbers come from?

From the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and an activity factor you pick. The page does not measure the calories you burn.

Which body-fat formula is this?

The US Navy circumference equation. Men use 86.010 log10(abdomen − neck) − 70.041 log10(height) + 36.76. Women use 163.205 log10(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 log10(height) − 78.387. Height and circumferences are in inches.
