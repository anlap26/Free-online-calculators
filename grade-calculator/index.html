Grade calculator

Each score is points earned divided by points possible, weighted, then mapped to A, B, C, D, or F.

The letters use 90, 80, 70, and 60. A school with a different cut will not match.

Each row is points earned, points possible, and a weight. The row's percent is earned divided by possible. That percent is multiplied by the weight. The result is the sum of those weighted percents divided by the sum of the weights, times 100. A blank weight counts as 1, so a single row with no weight is just earned divided by possible. Eighteen out of 20 with weight 40, and 45 out of 50 with weight 60, are both 90 percent. The weighted percent is (0.90×40 + 0.90×60) / 100, which is 90, and the letter is A. Forty-two out of 50 is 84 percent, which is a B. Fifty-nine out of 100 is an F. The cuts are 90 for A, 80 for B, 70 for C, and 60 for D. Anything under 60 is F. There is no A− and no plus.

Possible points have to be greater than zero. Earned points cannot be negative. A weight cannot be negative. A blank earned and a blank possible on the same row are skipped, so an unused row does not fail the form. Earned points may be larger than possible points. That is extra credit, and it can push the percent above 100. The letter is still A once the percent is at least 90. The page does not cap the percent at 100. If your course does, cap the earned points yourself before you type them.

This is not a GPA

The GPA page averages letters that already exist, weighted by credits, on a 4.0 map. This page starts from raw points and only then picks a letter. A 3.5 GPA is not fed back into this form, and an 84 percent is not reported as a 3.0. Those conversions belong to a school that published them. The percentage page will answer "84 is what percent of 100" and will not attach a letter. The fraction page will reduce 42/50 to 21/25 and will not call it a B. Use the page whose last line is the thing you were asked for.

Dropping the lowest score is not a switch. Delete that row if the syllabus says to drop it. A curve is not a switch either. If the instructor added points, type the points after the curve. The weights are relative. Weights of 40 and 60 are the same as weights of 2 and 3. Weights of 1 and 1 are an ordinary average of the two percents, not an average of the raw points. Eighteen out of 20 and 45 out of 50 are both 90 percent, so the unweighted average of the percents is 90. An average of the raw points, 63 out of 70, is also 90 in this particular example and is not the same operation in general. The page always averages the percents, then applies the weights.

What the cuts assume

  • 90, 80, 70, and 60 are the only letter boundaries.
  • A blank weight is 1, not zero. Zero would drop the row.
  • There is no extra-credit cap.

Letters you already have are on the GPA calculator. A percent without a letter is the percentage calculator. A reduced ratio is the fraction calculator.

Questions people ask

What letter is 42 out of 50?

84 percent, which is a B. The B band starts at 80 and stops before 90.

How do the weights work?

Each row is earned divided by possible, multiplied by its weight. The weights are summed, and the weighted percents are divided by that sum. A blank weight counts as 1.

What is 18/20 at weight 40 and 45/50 at weight 60?

Both rows are 90 percent, so the weighted percent is 90 and the letter is A.

Is this the same as the GPA calculator?

No. GPA averages letters you already have, using credits and a 4.0 map. This page starts from points and only then picks a letter on a 90/80/70/60 scale.
