Fraction calculator

Integer fractions, reduced to lowest terms, with a mixed number and a decimal beside the improper fraction.

Both denominators have to be non-zero integers. Dividing by a zero fraction is rejected.

Each fraction is a numerator over a denominator, and both have to be integers inside the safe integer range. A decimal such as 1.5 is rejected so the page does not quietly treat it as a float and then call it a fraction. A negative sign on the denominator is moved to the numerator before the operation, so 1 over −2 is the same fraction as −1 over 2. Addition uses the common denominator: a/b + c/d = (a×d + c×b) / (b×d). Subtraction changes the plus to a minus. Multiplication is a×c over b×d. Division by c/d is multiplication by d/c, and it is rejected when c is zero.

The result is reduced by the greatest common divisor of the numerator and the denominator. One half plus one third is three sixths plus two sixths, which is five sixths, and five and six have no common divisor above 1, so the improper fraction is 5/6. Two fourths times one is two fourths, which reduces to one half. Minus one half times three fourths is minus three eighths. The mixed-number line writes a whole part and a remainder when the absolute value is at least 1. Seven thirds is 2 and 1/3. Minus seven thirds is −2 and 1/3, with the remainder kept positive. A proper fraction shows no whole part. When the remainder is zero, the line is the integer alone.

Where the integer rule matters

Cross-multiplication of two large integers can leave the safe integer range, where a JavaScript number can no longer represent every integer. The page rejects that instead of returning a fraction that looks exact and is not. A zero denominator on either input is rejected before the arithmetic. The decimal line is the reduced numerator divided by the reduced denominator, and it is rounded for display. One third stays 1/3 on the fraction lines and a rounded 0.333... on the decimal line. If you need a percentage, divide on this page and move the decimal, or type the same relationship on the percentage page. The percentage page will not keep a repeating ratio as a fraction.

The triangle page uses ordinary lengths, not fractions. A 3-4-5 triangle has area 6, which you can also reach as a fraction if you want, but the triangle page does not reduce a ratio of sides. Standard deviation wants a list. This page wants four integers and an operation. There is no mixed-number input. Type the improper form, or convert a mixed number to an improper fraction before you type it: 2 and 1/3 is 7/3.

What the reducer does not do

  • It does not accept decimals, symbols, or blank denominators.
  • It does not simplify an expression with more than one operation.
  • The decimal is a display of the value, not a second exact form.

Percent questions are on the percentage calculator. Lengths and area are on the triangle calculator. A list of values is the standard deviation calculator.

Questions people ask

What is 1/2 plus 1/3?

5/6. The common denominator is 6, the numerators become 3 and 2, and 5 and 6 have no common divisor greater than 1.

How is a result reduced?

The numerator and the denominator are divided by their greatest common divisor. 2/4 becomes 1/2.

What does the mixed number show?

The whole part and a positive remainder. 7/3 is 2 and 1/3. −7/3 is −2 and 1/3. A proper fraction has no whole part.

Why are decimals rejected?

The page promises an integer fraction. A decimal would be a float, and calling the result a reduced fraction would overstate how exact it is.
