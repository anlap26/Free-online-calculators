The quote is the price you type, before the fill gets worse. On a buy, the execution price is the quote times (1 plus slippage). Slippage here is a percent you choose, not a measurement of the book. Notional is that execution price times the quantity. The fee is the notional times the fee rate. Buy total is the notional plus the fee: it is the cash that has to leave to own the coins. The effective price is the buy total divided by the quantity, so the fee is visible as a higher price per unit. A sell runs the same pieces with the sign flipped. Execution is the quote times (1 minus slippage), because a worse sell fills lower. The fee is still notional times the fee rate, and the cash you receive is the notional minus the fee.

A round trip and the quote that breaks even

The break-even sell quote is the buy total divided by the quantity, times (1 minus slippage), times (1 minus the exit fee). In symbols: buyTotal / (qty * (1 - slippage) * (1 - exitFee)). The exit is assumed to suffer the same slippage percent you typed, and the exit fee comes off the slipped notional. You are solving for the quote at which the cash coming back equals the cash that left. It is not a target a trader owes you. It is the flat line under the percents you entered.

Buy 2 units at a quote of 100, taker fee 0.1 percent, slippage zero. Notional is 200. The fee is 0.20. The total is 200.20. The effective price is 100.10. With zero slippage and 0.1 percent on both sides, the break-even sell quote is 100.10 divided by 0.999, about 100.20. The same figure is 200.20 divided by 2 times 0.999. The extra fraction above 100.10 is the fee you will pay on the way out. If you show a buy only, there is no break-even line, because the exit fee has not been chosen yet.

Maker, taker, and a discount

A taker order matches immediately against an order already resting on the book. You pay for a fill now, at the quote, plus whatever slippage you typed if the book is thin. A maker order sits on the book and waits for someone else to trade against it. It may not fill. Exchanges often charge these two roles different percents. You type both. The entry leg uses the percent of the role you picked for the entry, and the exit leg uses the role you picked for the exit. A fee discount, a higher tier, or a token offset changes the percent you type. It does not change the formula. This page does not know your tier and does not promise a discount amount.

Questions people ask

How do trading fees and slippage change the price I pay?

On a buy, the execution price is the quote times 1 plus the slippage percent. Notional is that price times quantity. The fee is the notional times the fee rate, and the cash out is notional plus fee. With 2 units at a quote of 100, a 0.1 percent taker fee, and no slippage, notional is 200, the fee is 0.20, and you pay 200.20. The effective price is 100.10. Slippage above zero raises the execution price before the fee.

How do you find the break-even sell price after fees?

Divide the cash you paid to buy by the quantity, then by 1 minus slippage, then by 1 minus the exit fee. With zero slippage and 0.1 percent on both sides, a buy at 100 that cost 100.10 per unit breaks even at 100.10 divided by 0.999, about 100.20. The exit is assumed to fill worse by the same slippage percent, and the exit fee comes off that slipped notional. The result is a flat line, not a price target.

What is the difference between a maker fee and a taker fee?

A taker order matches at once against an order already on the book, so you pay for a fill now. A maker order rests on the book and waits for someone else to trade against it, and it may never fill. Exchanges often charge the two roles different percents. You type both, and each leg uses the role you picked for it. A discount or a higher tier changes the percent you type. The multiplication stays notional times that rate.

Does a fee discount change the formula?

No. A discount, a volume tier, or a token offset changes the maker or taker percent you type into the form. The fee is still the notional times that rate, and the break-even quote still divides by 1 minus the exit fee. This page does not know which tier you are on and does not promise a discount amount. Read the schedule you were offered, including any expiry, because published tiers change.

Do I need an exchange account to calculate the fee?

No. Type the percents and the quote, and the cash figures are computed in the browser. An account at Bybit or Binance is one place to see the fee tier you were actually given. Those tiers change, and this page will not invent a discount. You can calculate from a schedule you already have and never sign up. The result is not a suggestion to place the trade.
