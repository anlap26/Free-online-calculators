Standard deviation calculator

The mean and both standard deviations of the numbers you type, with the sum of squared deviations left visible in the variance.

Population variance divides by the count. Sample variance divides by the count minus one.

Type the numbers separated by spaces, commas, or new lines. The count is how many numbers were read. The sum is their total. The mean is the sum divided by the count. Each deviation is a number minus the mean. The sum of squared deviations is what both variances share. Population variance divides that sum by the count. Population standard deviation is the square root. Sample variance divides by the count minus one, and it is reported only when there are at least two numbers. Sample standard deviation is the square root of that. One number has a population standard deviation of zero and no sample line, because a sample denominator of zero is not a variance.

The set 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 is the check. There are 8 numbers. The sum is 40. The mean is 5. The squared deviations are 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, and 16, which add to 32. Population variance is 32/8 = 4, and the population standard deviation is 2. Sample variance is 32/7. The page reports those values. Minimum and maximum are the smallest and largest numbers in the list, which for this set are 2 and 9. A blank list is rejected. A token that is not a number is rejected rather than skipped, so a stray word does not quietly shrink the count.

Which line to read

Use the population line when the numbers are the whole set you care about: every score in a class you are describing, every measurement you actually have and do not mean to generalize. Use the sample line when the numbers are a sample and the variance is meant to estimate a larger population. The sample divisor is the Bessel correction. It does not make the estimate unbiased for the standard deviation itself. It is the usual divisor for the variance. The page prints both so the choice stays on the page instead of inside a default you cannot see. Rounded display is not a change to the division. Very large lists are limited by what you can paste, not by a hidden cap in the formula.

A percentage question about two numbers is a different page. A fraction is a different page. This page will not tell you a percent change, and it will not reduce a ratio. If the numbers are a triangle's sides, the triangle page checks the triangle inequality and reports an area. The mean of three sides is not an area.

What the two divisors are not

  • The sample line is not "more accurate" for a complete set.
  • The population line is not wrong for a sample. It answers a different question.
  • Neither line is a confidence interval.

Two-number percent questions are on the percentage calculator. Integer ratios are on the fraction calculator. Three lengths are on the triangle calculator.

Questions people ask

What is the standard deviation of 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9?

The mean is 5. The sum of squared deviations is 32. The population standard deviation is 2, because 32/8 is 4 and the square root of 4 is 2. The sample variance is 32/7.

Why are there two standard deviations?

Population variance divides by the count. Sample variance divides by the count minus one. Use the sample line for a sample from a larger set, and the population line when the list is the whole set.

What happens with one number?

The mean is that number and the population standard deviation is zero. The sample line is withheld, because the sample divisor would be zero.

Are blank lines ignored?

Separators are ignored. A token that is not a number is an error, not a skipped item.
