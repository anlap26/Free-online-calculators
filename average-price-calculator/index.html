Average price calculator

Several buys, with fees in money, become one average cost and the sale quote that covers a percentage sell fee.

This is arithmetic for a plan. It is not a forecast and not investment, tax, or legal advice.

An exchange account is separate from the average

The average and the break-even are computed in the browser. Binance and Bybit are exchanges where people trade. Coins left there are in that company's custody. A hardware wallet is a different purchase. Fee schedules change. You can use this page without signing up.

Each row is one lot. The cost of a row is quantity times price, plus the fee on that row. That fee is an amount of money, not a percent. If the exchange showed you a percent, turn it into money before you type it, or the average will be wrong. The average price is (the sum of quantity times price, plus the fees) divided by the total quantity. Empty rows do not belong in the sum. A quantity of zero is rejected, because there is nothing to divide by. Overlapping lots are just more rows. Two buys at the same price are two lines. The formula adds them. It does not merge matching prices, and it does not care which row you typed first.

The sale quote that covers the sell fee

The break-even sale quote is the average divided by (1 minus the sell fee). The sell fee is a percent of the sale, not another money amount on the row. When that percent is zero, the break-even quote is the average itself. You need a quote high enough that, after the sale fee is taken off, what you receive still covers the average cost. A target sale price is optional. If you type one, profit or loss is the target minus the break-even quote, times the total quantity. Using the raw average there, and forgetting the sell fee, makes a winner look better than the cash you would actually get.

3200, and what averaging down really does

Buy 2 units at 3000 and 1 unit at 3600, with no fees. Cost is 6000 plus 3600, which is 9600. Quantity is 3. Average is 3200. Add a fee of 30 on the single unit and the cost becomes 9630, so the average is 3210. The fee is spread across every unit, because there is one average for the whole quantity. A sell fee of 1 percent on the no-fee case needs 3200 divided by 0.99, about 3232.32, before you are back to flat.

Averaging down does not jump the average to the new price. Suppose you hold 2 units at 3000, so the average is 3000, and you buy 1 more at 2400 with no fee. New cost is 8400. New quantity is 3. New average is 2800. The gap from the old average to the new price is 600. The new lot is one third of the new size, so the average moves one third of the way, by 200, and lands on 2800. A small buy barely moves a large position. A buy larger than what you already hold pulls the average past the midpoint, toward the new price, still in proportion to size.

What this average is not

  • It is not a forecast of the next trade. It is the cost of the lots you typed.
  • It does not read an order book or a live fee schedule.
  • A sale fee of zero means the break-even quote equals the average.
  • Taxes on a gain are outside the formula.
  • Coins left on an exchange are in that company's custody. A hardware wallet is a separate device for coins you withdraw.

The percent you pay to get in and out is a different question. The trading fee calculator takes a maker or taker percent and slippage and solves a round trip. Fee schedules change, so type the percent from the schedule you were offered. Binance and Bybit are exchanges where people trade the coins this form is often used for. Opening an account is optional. The average does not change if you never do. The Binance and Bybit guide describes what to compare, including custody, and it does not quote a fee that the firm can change next week. A hardware wallet is not included with the exchange account. Withdrawing to one is a separate decision, and this page does not price that device.

Questions people ask

How do you calculate the average price of several buys?

Multiply each quantity by its price, add the fee in money on that row, add the rows together, and divide by the total quantity. The fee on a row is cash, not a percent. Two buys at the same price are just two rows, and the order of the rows does not matter. The result is one cost for the whole pile. It is not a prediction of the next quote you will see.

Does averaging down reset my price to the latest buy?

No. The new average moves toward the new price in proportion to size. It does not jump there. If you hold 2 units at 3000 and buy 1 more at 2400 with no fee, the new average is 2800. The new lot is one third of the new quantity, so the average moves one third of the gap. A small extra buy barely moves a large position. Only a much larger buy pulls the average close to the new price.

How is the break-even sale price different from the average?

The average is your cost per unit, fees on the buys included. The break-even sale quote is that average divided by 1 minus the sell fee. The sell fee is a percent. With no sell fee, the two numbers match. Buy 2 at 3000 and 1 at 3600 with no fees and the average is 3200. A 1 percent sell fee needs a quote of about 3232.32 before the cash you receive covers that average.

Should the fee on each lot be a percent or an amount?

Type an amount of money on each lot. If the exchange showed a percent, convert it to money for that fill before you enter the row. The only percent on this page is the sale fee, and it is used solely for the break-even quote. Mixing those two boxes, a percent in the money field or cash in the percent field, moves the average or the sale quote for a reason that is not in the market.

Does the average price require an exchange account?

No. The page adds the lots in the browser. An exchange account is where a trade can happen, and coins left there stay in that company's custody. A hardware wallet is a separate device for coins you withdraw. It is not part of the exchange signup, and this average does not need either one. Binance and Bybit fee schedules change. The guide says what to compare. It does not quote a tariff that would be stale next week.
