Unit converter

Length, weight, temperature, and volume, converted through exact factors. A length is not converted into a weight.

The factors are exact definitions, not rounded approximations. Mixed dimensions are rejected.

Every conversion goes through a base unit. Lengths use the metre. One kilometre is 1,000 metres, one centimetre is 0.01 metres, one millimetre is 0.001 metres, one inch is 0.0254 metres, one foot is 0.3048 metres, one yard is 0.9144 metres, and one mile is 1,609.344 metres. One inch is therefore exactly 2.54 centimetres, and one mile is exactly 1.609344 kilometres. Weights use the kilogram. One pound is 0.45359237 kilograms and one ounce is 0.028349523125 kilograms. Volumes use the litre. One US gallon is 3.785411784 litres, one US quart is 0.946352946 litres, one US cup is 0.2365882365 litres, and one US fluid ounce is 0.0295735295625 litres. Those are the international and US customary definitions, not grocery-label roundings.

Temperature does not pass through a multiplicative base, because the zeros differ. Celsius to Fahrenheit is the Celsius value times 9/5, plus 32. Fahrenheit to Celsius subtracts 32 and multiplies by 5/9. Kelvin is Celsius plus 273.15. The checks are 32 Fahrenheit = 0 Celsius, 212 Fahrenheit = 100 Celsius, and 0 Celsius = 273.15 Kelvin. A negative Celsius temperature is allowed. There is no absolute-zero guard beyond what the arithmetic produces. Selecting two units from different families, such as kilograms and metres, is rejected. The page will not invent a density to connect them. Selecting the same unit twice returns the value unchanged.

Where these factors show up again

The BMI page uses 0.45359237 kilograms per pound and 0.0254 metres per inch, which are the same factors. The calorie page uses 2.54 centimetres per inch. The pace page uses 1.609344 kilometres per mile. If those pages and this one ever disagreed, one of them would be wrong. The pixel converter is a different kind of unit. A pixel is not a metre, and a rem depends on a root font size you type. That page stays separate so this one does not pretend a CSS pixel has a single physical length. Cooking cups in other countries are not the US cup stored here. A metric cup is not offered, because adding it under the same name would make the US cup look interchangeable with it.

Very large and very small values are ordinary floating-point numbers. The exact factors are exact as written. The product of a huge value and a factor can still round in the last bits of a JavaScript number. For everyday conversions that rounding is far past the digits the page shows. The display keeps up to eight digits after the decimal and drops trailing noise by the formatter. The operation itself is one multiplication and one division through the base, or the temperature affine step.

What is not in the list

  • No area, no speed, and no pressure. Pace has its own speed line.
  • No imperial gallon. The gallon here is the US gallon.
  • No stone, no tonne shortcut, and no temperature in Rankine.

Running pace is the pace calculator. Body mass uses the same pound and inch factors on the BMI calculator. CSS pixels are the PX to REM converter.

Questions people ask

How many centimetres are in an inch?

Exactly 2.54. The inch is 0.0254 metres and the centimetre is 0.01 metres.

How many kilometres are in a mile?

Exactly 1.609344. The mile is 1,609.344 metres.

What is 32 Fahrenheit in Celsius?

0. Water's boiling point, 212 Fahrenheit, is 100 Celsius. Zero Celsius is 273.15 Kelvin.

Can I convert kilograms to metres?

No. The page only converts inside length, inside weight, inside temperature, or inside volume.
