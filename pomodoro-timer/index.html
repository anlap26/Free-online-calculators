The timer does not need a tunnel

A cafe network is a fair reason to use a VPN while you work. NordVPN is one such tunnel. The timer does not depend on it, does not check your network, and does not store the session.

See NordVPN

The timer is a focus block, a short break, and a long break. The long break arrives after a number of focus blocks you set. The fields start at 25 minutes of focus, 5 minutes of short break, 15 minutes of long break, and 4 focus blocks before the long break. Change any of them before you start, or while you are paused on that phase. The labels on the page follow the phase you are in. Nothing about the plan is stored. Refresh the page and you are back to whatever the fields say now, with the count of finished focus blocks at zero.

The clock uses a timestamp

When you press start, the page notes the moment the phase should end. Each tick asks the clock what time it is and subtracts. A naive counter would subtract a quarter second every quarter second and drift when the browser slows a background tab. This one recomputes the time left from the end timestamp, so a tab that slept and woke tends to show the right remaining time instead of a count that kept ticking in the dark. It is still a page timer. A computer that sleeps entirely, or a browser that freezes the tab until you return, will correct when the tick runs again. It is not a hardware alarm, and it will not buzz a phone that is locked in another room.

A short beep uses the Web Audio API, and only after you have pressed start. Browsers block audio that a page tries to play before a gesture. The start button is that gesture. The tone is brief. If the browser refuses the audio context, the clock still moves and the beep is simply absent. There is no soundtrack and no account tied to the sound.

What the buttons do

Start begins or resumes the current phase. Pause keeps the time left and stops the clock. Skip ends the phase early and moves on: a finished focus block counts toward the long break, and a break returns you to focus. Reset clears the finished count, returns to focus, and puts the full focus length back on the clock. The long break is chosen when the number of finished focus blocks divides evenly by the block count you set. With the default of 4, the long break follows the fourth, the eighth, and so on. The short break follows the others.

The timer does not make the work billable. Sitting through four focus blocks is not a rate, a tax gross-up, or a count of hours a client will pay. Utilization, days you cannot bill, and the hourly figure that falls out of them live on the freelance rate calculator. If the work was writing, the text analyzer can count the draft afterwards. The timer will not attach itself to that draft.

A cafe network is a separate decision

Working from a cafe is a reasonable reason to put the laptop on a tunnel. The timer does not depend on that tunnel. It runs the same on a home connection, on a cafe network, and with no network at all once the page has loaded. NordVPN is one VPN people use for an untrusted network. This page does not test your connection, does not rank VPN products, and does not claim the beep is safer because a tunnel is on. Turn a tunnel on for the network. Leave it off if you do not need it. The clock does not care.

Questions people ask

What are the default lengths?

25 minutes of focus, 5 minutes of short break, 15 minutes of long break, and a long break after 4 focus blocks. You can change each number.

Why does a background tab not lose as much time?

The page stores the moment the phase should end and recomputes the time left from the clock. A counter that only subtracts ticks drifts more when the tab is slowed.

When does the beep play?

After you have pressed start, using the Web Audio API, when a phase ends. If the browser blocks audio, the clock still runs.

Is the session stored?

No. Refresh the page and the finished count is gone. There is no account.

Does the timer make the time billable?

No. It only runs the blocks. The freelance rate calculator is where utilization and the hour are priced.
