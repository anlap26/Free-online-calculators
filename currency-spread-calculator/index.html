Currency spread calculator

The amount a bank rate delivers, set against the interbank rate you type, plus an alternative fee you can compare.

A specialist can cost less, or not

The gap on this page is the difference between the two rates you typed. Wise is one specialist for cross-border transfers. It can be cheaper than the bank or it can be not, depending on the route and the fee you type. This page does not know Wise's current fee, and it works from the bank quote alone.

Open Wise

You type both rates. The page does not fetch a live quote, and the currency names are labels on the result. They do not look up a market. Point both rates the same way: units of the currency you receive for one unit you give. The bank delivers the amount times the bank rate. The interbank rate delivers the amount times the interbank rate. The loss is the mid amount minus the bank amount. The percent is that loss divided by the mid amount. Basis points are the percent times 10,000. If you invert one rate and not the other, the gap is a nonsense number. The form cannot tell that you swapped the direction.

1000 at 1.10 and 1.07

Convert 1000. The interbank rate is 1.10 and the bank rate is 1.07. The mid amount is 1100. The bank delivers 1070. The loss is 30. As a percent of the mid amount that is 30 divided by 1100, about 2.73 percent, which is about 273 basis points. Nothing in that chain is a forecast of tomorrow's rate. It is the two numbers you typed, multiplied and subtracted. A bank that shows you only its own rate has not shown you this gap. You still have to bring the interbank figure from somewhere you trust, and you should note the time, because both rates move.

An alternative fee is a percent of the mid amount

The alternative delivers the mid amount times (1 minus the fee you type). The fee is a percent of the mid amount, not of the 1000 you started with. A fee of 0.5 percent on a mid amount of 1100 is 5.50, so the alternative delivers 1094.50. That is 24.50 more than the bank in this example. Type a larger fee and the alternative can fall below the bank. The comparison is arithmetic, not a ranking of companies. This 0.5 percent is a number for the example. It is not a Wise tariff. The page does not know Wise's current fee. That fee changes with the route, the amount, and the day, and inventing it here would be a false quote.

How to read the sign

  • A positive loss means the bank delivers less than the interbank rate you typed.
  • A negative loss means the bank rate you typed is richer than that mid. Check that the boxes are not swapped.
  • Both rates must be in the same direction. Names of currencies do not flip them.
  • The page does not call a rate service. A stale pair of rates makes a stale gap.
  • Inflation and a city's cost index are different losses. They are not this percent.

Questions people ask

How do you calculate the hidden fee in a bank exchange rate?

Multiply the amount by the interbank rate and by the bank rate. The loss is the mid amount minus what the bank delivers. The percent is that loss divided by the mid amount. Convert 1000 at an interbank rate of 1.10 and a bank rate of 1.07. The mid amount is 1100, the bank delivers 1070, and the loss is 30, about 2.73 percent, or about 273 basis points. You have to type both rates.

What is a basis point on this currency page?

A basis point is one hundredth of a percent. The page takes the loss as a fraction of the mid amount and multiplies by 10,000. A gap of about 2.73 percent is about 273 basis points. The money loss and the percent describe the same gap. Basis points are only a finer way to read the percent. They are not a separate fee the bank added on top, and they are only as current as the two rates you typed.

How does the alternative fee compare with the bank?

The alternative keeps the mid amount after a percent you type. That percent applies to the mid amount, not to the sum you started with. On a mid amount of 1100, a 0.5 percent fee removes 5.50 and leaves 1094.50, which is 24.50 more than the bank's 1070 in the example. A larger fee can leave less than the bank. The 0.5 percent is an example input, not a firm's current tariff.

Why might the loss come out negative?

The loss is the mid amount minus the bank amount. If the bank rate you typed is higher than the interbank rate, the bank delivers more and the loss is negative. That can mean a generous quote, or it can mean the two boxes were swapped. Both rates have to point the same way: units received per one unit you give. The currency names do not flip a rate.

Does this page know what Wise charges today?

No. Wise is one specialist for cross-border transfers, and on a given route it can be cheaper than the bank or it can be not, once you include the fee. This calculator does not store Wise's current fee, because that fee changes with the corridor, the amount, and the day. Type the fee you were actually quoted into the alternative box. The arithmetic works from the bank rate and the interbank rate alone if that is all you have.
