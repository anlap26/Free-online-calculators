BMI calculator

Weight divided by height squared, with adult WHO categories and the weight that would land between 18.5 and 24.9.

BMI is a ratio for adults. It is not a diagnosis, and the categories are not cut points for children.

BMI is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. A person who weighs 70 kilograms and is 1.75 metres tall has a BMI of 70 divided by 1.75 squared. 1.75 squared is 3.0625, and 70 divided by 3.0625 is about 22.9, which sits in the adult range the World Health Organization calls healthy: 18.5 up to, but not including, 25. Under 18.5 is labelled underweight, 25 up to 30 is labelled overweight, and 30 or more is labelled obese. Those words are the names of the cut points. They are not a reading of muscle, bone, age, pregnancy, or a lab result.

Pounds and inches go through exact factors before the division, so the index does not depend on which unit system you pick. One pound is 0.45359237 kilograms. One inch is 0.0254 metres. A weight of 70 divided by 0.45359237 pounds, and a height of 175 divided by 2.54 inches, is the same body as 70 kilograms and 175 centimetres, and the BMI matches to rounding error. The page does not use the rounded shortcut of 703 times pounds divided by inches squared, because that shortcut and the exact factors disagree in the fourth digit. The healthy-weight line uses BMI 18.5 at the bottom and 24.9 at the top, which is the last value still inside the healthy band before 25.

How to read the range

The range line is the weight, in the units you picked, that would produce a BMI of 18.5 and a BMI of 24.9 at the height you typed. It moves only when the height moves. It is not a target a clinician set, and it is not adjusted for age or frame. Change the height and both ends of the range change with the square of the height. Change only the weight and the BMI changes, but the range stays put. A height of zero or a weight of zero is rejected, because the ratio would be undefined or zero for a reason that is not a body.

Children and teenagers are scored against age-specific charts this page does not contain. An athlete with more muscle can sit in the overweight band with a low body-fat percentage. The body-fat page uses circumferences and will not agree with BMI, because it is a different formula. Pregnancy is outside both. If you need calories rather than an index, the calorie page uses weight, height, age, and an activity factor. BMI uses only weight and height.

What the index leaves out

  • No age, sex, waist, or body-fat input enters the ratio.
  • The categories are adult WHO cut points, not paediatric percentiles.
  • A high BMI does not by itself say why the weight is high.
  • Switching units does not change the mathematics, only the labels on the inputs.

Daily calories from the Mifflin–St Jeor equation are on the calorie calculator. A circumference estimate is on the body fat calculator. Pace from a distance and a time is on the pace calculator. None of the three reads a scale or a tape. You type the measurements.

Questions people ask

How is BMI calculated?

Divide weight in kilograms by height in metres squared. 70 kilograms at 1.75 metres is 70 / 3.0625, about 22.9.

Do pounds and kilograms give the same BMI?

Yes. Pounds are multiplied by 0.45359237 and inches by 0.0254 before the division. The same body in either system matches.

What do the categories mean?

They are adult WHO bands: under 18.5, 18.5 to 24.9, 25 to 29.9, and 30 or more. They are not a diagnosis and they are not cut points for children.

Why does the healthy range use 24.9 and not 25?

The overweight band starts at 25. The top of the healthy band on this page is 24.9, the last published value before that cut.
