Body fat calculator

The US Navy circumference equation, in inches, with a base-10 logarithm. Centimetres are divided by 2.54 before the log.

This is a circumference equation, not a scan and not a medical measurement.

The men's equation is 86.010 times the base-10 log of abdomen minus neck, minus 70.041 times the base-10 log of height, plus 36.76. The women's equation is 163.205 times the base-10 log of waist plus hip minus neck, minus 97.684 times the base-10 log of height, minus 78.387. Height and the circumferences are in inches. If you type centimetres, each value is divided by 2.54 before those logs, which is why the same tape in both unit systems agrees. The logs are undefined when the value inside is zero or negative, so abdomen must be greater than neck for men, and waist plus hip must be greater than neck for women.

These are the circumference equations used for the US Navy body-composition estimate. They are not the Siri density form, and they will not match a calculator that uses 495 divided by a density minus 450. A man who is 70 inches tall, with a 15 inch neck and a 34 inch abdomen, is 86.010 × log10(19) − 70.041 × log10(70) + 36.76, about 17.5 percent. A woman who is 64 inches tall, with a 13 inch neck, a 30 inch waist, and 38 inch hips, is 163.205 × log10(55) − 97.684 × log10(64) − 78.387, about 29.2 percent. Type those centimetres, which are the inches times 2.54, and the percentage matches.

How the tape is supposed to be read

The Navy instructions measure the men's abdomen horizontally at the navel, the neck just below the larynx, the women's waist at the narrowest point, and the hips at the widest point, all in inches, with the person standing. This page cannot see whether you measured those landmarks. It applies the equation to the numbers you type. A tape pulled tighter changes the result, and the equation will not tell you that the tape was tight. Body fat from a DEXA scan, a caliper fold, or a bioimpedance scale is a different method and will not reproduce this number.

BMI does not use a tape, so the two pages can disagree without either one having a bug. The calorie page does not use a tape either. Age is not an input here. A result that comes out negative or far above a plausible range means the circumferences are inconsistent with the equation, not that the logarithm invented a body. Check that the units match the numbers: 70 inches is not 70 centimetres.

What the equation leaves out

  • It does not see muscle, bone, or a scan.
  • It is an adult operational estimate, not a paediatric chart.
  • The women's equation needs the hip. The men's equation ignores it.
  • Switching unit systems is a conversion, not a second formula.

Weight and height alone are the BMI calculator. An energy estimate is the calorie calculator. A run is the pace calculator.

Questions people ask

Which body-fat formula is this?

The US Navy circumference equation. Men: 86.010×log10(abdomen − neck) − 70.041×log10(height) + 36.76. Women: 163.205×log10(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684×log10(height) − 78.387. Measurements are in inches.

Why do centimetres match inches?

Each centimetre value is divided by 2.54 before the logarithm. The equation itself is defined in inches.

Why was my result rejected?

The value inside the logarithm has to be positive. For men the abdomen must be larger than the neck. For women the waist plus the hip must be larger than the neck.

Will this match a DEXA scan?

Not in general. A scan, a caliper, and this circumference equation are different methods. They are not required to print the same percentage.
