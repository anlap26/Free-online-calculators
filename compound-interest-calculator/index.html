The rate box is a nominal annual rate. It is not a monthly rate, and it is not an APY that already has compounding inside it. The calculator builds a monthly rate with i = (1 + rate/n)^(n/12) - 1. Here n is how many times a year the stated rate compounds. When n is 12, the exponent is 1, so i is simply rate/12. Twelve percent compounded monthly is one percent a month. When n is 1, twelve monthly steps rebuild one annual compound, which is not the same thing as dividing the annual rate by 12. When n is 4, each quarter is rate/4, and the monthly rate is that quarterly step spread across twelve months. If the number you found is already an effective yearly yield, use the staking APY calculator and mark it as APY. Putting an APY into this nominal box compounds the compounding.

End of the month and start of the month

P is the starting amount. PMT is the sum added each month. m is the years you typed, times 12, rounded to a whole month. End-of-month future value is FV = P*(1+i)^m + PMT*((1+i)^m - 1)/i. The first term grows money already there. The second term is the annuity, the row of contributions. Each one arrives after that month's growth, so the last contribution earns nothing inside the window. Start-of-month timing multiplies the annuity by (1+i). The principal is not given an extra month on top of the growth it already has. A contribution at the start of the month is inside the balance when that month's rate is applied, which is why the result is a little higher. A zero rate sets the formula aside. Future value is then the principal plus every contribution.

Two balances you can recompute

Take 1000 at 10 percent compounded once a year for 2 years, and add nothing each month. After one year the balance is 1100. After two years it is 1210. The page reports 1210, because 1.10 squared is 1.21. The monthly rate under annual compounding is smaller than rate/12, and twenty-four of those steps still land on 1210.

Start from nothing and add 100 a month, at 12 percent nominal compounded monthly, for 12 months, at the end of the month. The monthly rate is 0.01. You contribute 1200. The annuity comes to about 1268.25. The extra 68.25 is interest on the payments that arrived earlier. The same inputs at the start of the month come to about 1280.93, the end-of-month figure times 1.01. A zero rate returns 1200 from a zero start, or the principal plus 1200 if you began with a balance. Both timings match when the rate is zero, because there is no growth to move.

What the run leaves out

  • One rate covers the whole period. A later change needs a new run.
  • Taxes, a withheld distribution, and an account fee are not subtracted.
  • Inflation is a separate assumption. A larger balance can still buy less.
  • Months are whole months. One and a half years is 18 contributions.
  • The rate you type is your assumption. It is not a return an account has promised.

Questions people ask

How do you calculate compound interest with monthly deposits?

Type a nominal annual rate and how many times a year it compounds. The monthly rate is (1 + rate/n) raised to n/12, minus 1, which is the annual rate divided by 12 when compounding is monthly. Each month grows the balance, then an end-of-month deposit is added. The starting amount and the deposits are shown apart from the growth. The rate stays constant for the whole period you typed.

What is the difference between start-of-month and end-of-month deposits?

End-of-month deposits arrive after that month's growth, so the last deposit earns nothing inside the window. Start-of-month deposits are in the balance when the month's rate is applied. Only the row of deposits is multiplied by one extra month of growth. The starting amount is not given a second extra month. With 100 a month at 12 percent compounded monthly for a year, the end figure is about 1268.25 and the start figure is about 1280.93.

Is a nominal annual rate the same as APY?

No. A nominal rate still has to be compounded by the count you pick. An APY is already the effective yearly yield, compounding included. If you type an APY into the nominal box, the calculator compounds it again and the balance runs too high. Use the staking calculator when the figure you have is an APY, and mark that mode. This page wants the rate before that yearly compounding is baked in.

Does this compound interest calculator include taxes?

No. The future value is the balance under the rate and the deposits you typed. A tax on gains, a withheld distribution, and a broker commission are not subtracted. Inflation is not subtracted either. A larger nominal balance can buy less, and that question has its own page. Run the numbers again if the rate will not stay constant, because this form has a single rate for the whole period.

Do I need a brokerage account to use the result?

No. The future value is calculated in the browser from the fields you fill in. A brokerage account is only a place to hold contributions if you decide to invest. Interactive Brokers is one broker people use across countries, and fees and residency rules change. A crypto purchase on an exchange is a different kind of account, with custody risk. Neither link changes the arithmetic, and the rate you typed is not a promised return.
