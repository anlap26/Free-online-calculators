The draft is assembled from what you type and what you tick. The pieces are the site name, the URL, the contact email, the country, and the date. The boxes cover analytics, cookies, affiliate links, accounts, a contact path, and whether a page calls a public IP service. Each box adds or withholds a paragraph. Leave a box off and the draft says the matching thing is not happening, which is only safe if that is actually true. Turn a box on and the draft says the thing may happen, and it leaves a blank where you still have to name the provider.

One paragraph is always there: calculations that stay in the browser are not sent to the publisher. The draft says numbers typed into the finance tools, the password generator, the encryptor, and the strength check stay on the device. That sentence matches a site built the way this one is built. If your site posts those fields to a server, the sentence is false, and you should delete it. The draft tells the reader to delete any sentence that is not true. Read it as an editor, not as a customer.

What the draft is not

It is not legal advice, and it is not a GDPR certificate. Ticking a country does not register you with a regulator, does not appoint a representative, and does not list a lawful basis in the language a lawyer would use for your facts. A real policy has to match the real processors. If you turn analytics on, name the counter and link to that company's own policy. If a payment firm, an email host, or a support desk holds data, the draft does not know their names until you write them in. The IP box mentions the services this site's IP page calls. If your page calls different ones, replace the names.

Accounts, if you tick them, produce a reminder to describe what you store, where, and for how long. The reminder is not that description. A contact path says that a message contains what the person wrote and is used to reply. It does not create the mailbox. The affiliate box says a partner can see that a click arrived through an affiliate link and may set a cookie on the partner's own site, and that the price does not go up because of the link. If your site has no affiliate links, leave the box off.

Read this site's own pages before you copy the shape

This site publishes its own privacy policy and its own affiliate disclosure. Those pages describe this site. They are not a template you can stamp on a shop, and the generator is the closer tool when the site is yours. Copying this site's policy onto a different product would make sentences that are not true. The generator exists so the sentences follow your boxes instead.

Questions people ask

Is the draft legal advice?

No. It is text assembled from your boxes. It is not a legal opinion and not a GDPR certificate.

What if a sentence is not true of my site?

Delete it. The draft says so. A box you leave off writes that the thing is not happening, which is only right when that is true.

Are calculator inputs sent to the publisher?

The draft says calculations that stay in the browser are not sent. If your site posts those fields to a server, delete that sentence.

Does the tool name my processors?

No. A real policy has to match the real processors. If you enable analytics, you still have to name the counter and link to its policy.

Where is this site's own policy?

This site's privacy policy and affiliate disclosure are separate pages. They describe this site, not yours.
