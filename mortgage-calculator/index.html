Mortgage calculator

A fixed-rate home loan turned into a monthly principal-and-interest payment, with tax, insurance, and HOA kept on their own lines.

This is the amortizing-loan formula for a rate you type. It is not a loan offer, a tax deduction, or an approval.

The monthly interest rate is the annual rate you type, divided by twelve. That is the convention for a fixed-rate home loan in the United States. It is not the effective monthly rate you would get by raising one plus the annual rate to the power of one twelfth. The number of payments is the years you type, times twelve, rounded to a whole month. The principal is the home price minus the down payment. The principal-and-interest payment is principal times the monthly rate times one plus that rate, raised to the number of payments, divided by that same power minus one. A zero rate skips the power formula, because it would divide by zero, and the payment is the principal divided by the number of payments.

Property tax and insurance are the yearly amounts you type, divided by twelve. A monthly HOA or similar fee is added as you typed it. None of those three changes the interest. The monthly payment line adds them to principal and interest. Each month the interest portion is the remaining balance times the monthly rate, and the rest of the contractual payment reduces the balance. The last payment is whatever balance remains, so the schedule ends at zero rather than a leftover cent. The year table groups those months and shows that year's interest, that year's principal, and the balance at the end of the year.

A price you can recompute

Take a home priced at 400,000 with 80,000 down. The amount borrowed is 320,000. At 6.5 percent for 30 years there are 360 payments, and the monthly rate is 0.065 divided by 12. Put that rate into the payment formula. Tax of 3,600 a year is 300 a month. Insurance of 1,200 a year is 100 a month. With the HOA box at zero, the monthly payment line is principal and interest plus 400. A larger down payment does not change the rate. It changes the principal the rate is applied to. If the down payment is larger than the price, there is nothing to amortize and the page asks you to check the numbers.

A second check uses a round loan and no extras. Borrow 200,000 at 6 percent for 30 years and set tax, insurance, and HOA to zero. The monthly rate is 0.005. One plus that rate, raised to 360, is about 6.022575. The payment is 200,000 times 0.005 times that power, divided by the power minus one, which comes to about 1,199.10. Over 360 payments the interest is those payments minus 200,000. A zero rate on 1,200 borrowed for one year is exactly 100 a month and zero interest. Both of those cases are what the formula produces. They are not a quote from a particular lender.

What the schedule leaves out

  • The rate is fixed for the whole term. An adjustable rate, a teaser period, or a refinance needs a new run.
  • Closing costs, discount points, and mortgage insurance are not added to the balance.
  • Tax and insurance are the amounts you typed. The page does not look up a mill rate or a premium.
  • Extra monthly payments belong on the loan calculator. This schedule assumes the contractual payment only.
  • The result is arithmetic. It is not an offer, a deduction, or a statement of what a lender will approve.

A personal or auto loan, with an optional extra payment, is the loan calculator. Interest that does not compound is the simple interest calculator. A balance that grows because you add money, rather than a balance you pay down, is the compound interest calculator. What the same money buys after inflation is the inflation calculator. You type the rate you were offered, or the rate you want to test. The page does not read a rate sheet.

Questions people ask

How is the monthly mortgage rate calculated?

The annual rate you type is divided by 12. A 6 percent loan uses a monthly rate of 0.005. That is the US fixed-rate convention, not the effective rate from compounding the annual rate monthly.

Does the payment include tax and insurance?

Principal and interest are on their own line. Tax per month is the yearly tax you typed, divided by 12. Insurance is handled the same way. HOA is the monthly amount you typed. The total adds the four lines.

Why is the last payment different?

Each month interest is the remaining balance times the monthly rate. The last payment is sized to the balance that is left, so the schedule ends at zero instead of a leftover cent.

Can I use this for an adjustable-rate mortgage?

Only for the period where the rate you typed actually applies. When the rate changes, run the remaining balance for the remaining months at the new rate.
