The conversion rate in the form is the rate you have now, on the slower site. Revenue now is visits times that rate times the value of one conversion. Extra seconds are the current load minus the faster load, and only when that difference is positive. If the faster number is not actually faster, extra seconds are zero and the two revenues match. Each extra second is assumed to have multiplied conversion by one minus the loss. Revenue if faster is revenue now divided by that factor raised to the extra seconds. The gap is the faster revenue minus the revenue now. The page also shows the gap times 12, as a flat year of the same month, not a forecast.

The loss field starts at 7 percent because the form needs a number. That 7 percent is an assumption you can edit. It is not a law. Published studies do not agree on one percent of conversion lost per second, and this page does not pick a winner among them or quote a study as if it were your shop. If you have your own before-and-after, type that loss. If you do not, treat the default as a dial, not as a measurement of your visitors.

A worked example

Ten thousand visits, a conversion of 2 percent, and 50 per conversion give revenue now of 10000. Move from 4 seconds to 2 seconds and the extra time is 2 seconds. At a loss of 7 percent the kept factor per second is 0.93, and 0.93 raised to 2 is 0.8649. Faster revenue is 10000 divided by 0.8649, which is about 11562. The gap is about 1562 in that month. Replace the 7 with another loss and both the factor and the gap change. The visits, the rate, and the value are yours as well. The page did not observe them.

This page does not time your site

There is no probe, no lab run, and no field metric. You type the seconds. A figure you copied from a report is only as good as that report, and a figure you guessed is a guess. Milliseconds are not invented here to make a host look quicker. If you do not know the two load times, the revenue gap is not ready to be believed.

A faster host does not fix a huge image. If the heavy part of the page is a picture, start with the image compressor, which resizes and re-encodes in the browser and shows the bytes you actually saved on that file. The speed model only becomes a story about images when the image was the heavy part. Contrast and type size are separate checks. They do not move the seconds in this form.

Questions people ask

Is the 7 percent a real finding?

No. It is the starting value of an assumption you can edit. Published studies do not agree on one percent per second, and this page does not pick one.

Which conversion rate do I type?

The rate you have now, on the slower site. The formula treats each extra second as having multiplied that rate by one minus the loss.

Does the page measure my load time?

No. You type both times. There is no probe and no invented millisecond.

Will a faster host fix a large image?

No. If the image is the heavy part, compress that file. A host change does not resize it.

What does the example come to?

Ten thousand visits at 2 percent and 50 each is 10000 now. From 4 seconds to 2 seconds at 7 percent loss, faster revenue is about 11562 and the gap is about 1562.
