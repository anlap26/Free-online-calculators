The index, and the salary it scales

Equivalent salary equals the salary you type, multiplied by the second index, divided by the first. In symbols, salary times index B, divided by index A. A salary of 4000 at index 100 versus index 68 is 2720, because 4000 times 68 divided by 100 is 2720. That 2720 is the pay in the second city that sits on the same place in the index. It is not a job offer, and it is not what a landlord will put in an advert. The page also divides the same salary by each index, so you can see one sum against each city's points. At 4000 and 100 that is 40 per point. The salary is in one currency for both cities. The index does not convert money. If the two places would pay you in different currencies, convert first, with a rate you choose, and then scale.

The file describes the basket as a mix of rent, food, and local transport. The scaling moves that whole basket. It does not price a specific flat. A two-room place near a metro and a room an hour out can sit far apart while the city still has one index. Rent can diverge from the basket. A city can be moderate on food and transport and tight on housing, or the other way round. One advert you saved is evidence about that flat. It is not a correction of the index until you decide to edit the file.

  • London is 90. Singapore is 85. Dubai is 78.
  • Limassol is 70. Berlin is 68. Lisbon is 64.
  • Bangkok is 42. Tbilisi is 38.
  • Same index in both cities means the scaled salary equals the salary you typed.

What the index leaves out

Tax is not applied. If you type a gross figure, the scaled figure is gross in the same sense. If you type a net figure, the scaled figure is net. The ratio does not know your bracket. A paycheck after tax, pension, and local contributions is a different sum, and this form will not invent those rates. The freelance rate calculator is the page that grosses up a target net for a tax percent you type yourself. It still does not look up a national table.

Price change over time is a different tool. The inflation calculator applies a rate to a currency across years. This index compares two places on one reviewed date. A salary can look comfortable after you move it to a lower index and still buy less later if the currency you are paid in loses purchasing power. Keep the two questions apart. The FIRE calculator takes yearly spending and a withdrawal rate you type and turns them into a portfolio figure. That is a third question. It does not read this index. Days in a country, if that is the limit you are watching, belong on the Schengen stay calculator.

Healthcare cost is outside the index. A routine doctor, a dentist, a prescription, and a premium are not lines in the basket of rent, food, and local transport. The index cannot tell you what a visit costs or what a policy pays. Genki is the nomad medical name in the Genki guide, which is where that plan is discussed. The guide does not quote a premium. Questions people ask

Is this an official rent survey or a Numbeo scrape?

Neither. The figures are a rounded editorial index with New York at 100, last reviewed on 19 November 2026. They live in assets/js/cost-of-living.js. They were not taken from a statistical office, and they were not scraped from Numbeo. The basket in that file is a mix of rent, food, and local transport. When you have a source you trust more, replace the number in the file. Change the review note in the same file so the date stays honest.

Why does a 4000 salary at 100 versus 68 become 2720?

The scaled salary is the salary multiplied by the second index and divided by the first. Four thousand times 68, divided by 100, is 2720. That is the pay at index 68 which sits where 4000 sits at index 100. It is not an offer and not a rent quote. The page also divides the salary by each index. At 4000 and 100 that is 40 per point. Both cities use the one currency you have in mind.

Does the index tell me the rent of a flat?

No. The formula scales a whole basket. It does not price a specific flat. Rent can diverge from the basket: a city can be moderate on food and transport and still be tight on housing, or the reverse. An advert you saved is evidence about that flat, not a new index, until you choose to edit assets/js/cost-of-living.js. Two cities with the same index produce a scaled salary equal to the salary you typed.

How is this different from the inflation calculator?

This index compares two places on one reviewed date. The inflation calculator applies a rate you type to a currency across time. A lower index can make the same salary look larger and still leave you exposed if that currency buys less later. The pages do not share a number. Tax is outside this index. A gross salary scales as gross, and a net salary scales as net. The ratio does not know your bracket.

Does the index include a doctor or an insurance premium?

No. Healthcare sits outside the basket of rent, food, and local transport. The index cannot tell you the price of a visit or what a policy pays. Genki is the nomad medical name in the guide, and the guide says what to read in that plan. Neither page quotes a premium or an exclusion. Those terms change. Read the current plan for the country you will actually be in, and keep the index as a planning ratio.
