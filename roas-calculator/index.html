ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend. Type both numbers in the same currency and for the same period. If spend is zero the ratio is not a useful finite number, so do not read a blank campaign as an infinite success. Gross profit is revenue times the margin. The margin is the share of revenue you keep before ads. A margin of 1, shown as 100 percent, means you typed no cost of goods, so the whole revenue is still there before the ad bill. A margin of one half means half the revenue is gone to the product before any ad is paid. Profit is that gross figure minus ad spend minus the other marketing cost you type. ROMI is profit divided by spend plus that other cost. The other cost can be zero.

A worked example

Spend 1000, revenue 4000, margin 100 percent, other cost 0. ROAS is 4000 divided by 1000, which is 4. Gross profit is the full 4000, because the margin keeps all of the revenue. Profit is 4000 minus 1000 minus 0, which is 3000. ROMI is 3000 divided by 1000, which is 300 percent. The same revenue at a margin of 50 percent changes the story. Gross profit is 2000. Profit is 2000 minus 1000, which is 1000. Break-even ROAS is 2, because you need 2 of revenue per 1 of spend to cover a 50 percent margin: half of that revenue is the gross you keep, and that half has to equal the spend. At a ROAS of 4 with a 50 percent margin you are above that line. At a ROAS of 4 with a thin margin you may not be.

Break-even is the spend, plus any other cost, divided by the margin, and then divided by the spend to turn it back into a ROAS. If the margin is zero, there is no finite break-even, because no amount of revenue leaves a gross profit. The page will not invent a healthy ratio to hide that.

Attribution is an assumption

The revenue you type is the revenue you decided to attribute to the ads. The form cannot see your shop, your call log, or the clicks that never bought. A last-click rule, a first-click rule, and a rule that shares credit across a week will put different numbers in the same box, and all of them are assumptions. If two campaigns share a customer, typing the full order against both will double the revenue. The calculator will happily divide whatever you typed. It will not warn you that the same order was counted twice, because it cannot see the order.

Labels on the click, if you use them, are built in the UTM and QR tool. They do not fill this form. The gap between a price and the cost of the goods, if you need it before you choose a margin, is the margin and markup calculator. People mix those two percentages and then type the wrong share here.

Questions people ask

What is ROAS?

Revenue divided by ad spend. Spend 1000 and revenue 4000 is a ROAS of 4.

What does the margin field mean?

It is the share of revenue kept before ads. One, or 100 percent, means you typed no cost of goods. One half means half the revenue is gone before ads.

Why is break-even ROAS 2 at a 50 percent margin?

You need 2 of revenue per 1 of spend so the half you keep covers the spend. That is with no other marketing cost.

Does the calculator know which orders came from the ads?

No. The revenue you type is your attribution. Two rules can put two different numbers in the same box.

What is ROMI here?

Profit divided by ad spend plus the other marketing cost. Profit is revenue times margin, minus spend, minus that other cost.
