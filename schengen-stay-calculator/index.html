This calculator stores every calendar day of the trips you list, and it stores each day only once. If two trips share a date, that date is not added twice, and the order of the rows does not matter. Schengen mode looks at a rolling window and compares the days inside it with 90. Year mode counts the distinct days of those trips that fall in the calendar year you name. Both numbers are a planning aid. A border decision, a visa, and a tax office sit outside the form.

The 180-day window

The Schengen window is the day you choose plus the previous 179 days. That span is 180 days, and the chosen day is included. The page counts how many stored dates fall inside the span and sets the count beside 90. A stay from 1 January 2026 through 31 March 2026 is 90 days. The year 2026 is not a leap year: January has 31 days, February has 28, and March has 31. On 31 March every one of those days is inside the window, so the window is full, and it has not gone over. If the same stay continues and 1 April is also a day present, the count becomes 91, and 91 is over the line.

Entry and exit both count. A trip from Monday through Wednesday is three calendar days, even if the hotel was two nights. Weekends and public holidays count, and there is no switch that drops Saturday. If the end date is earlier than the start, the row is rejected. Weekdays between two dates belong on the business days calculator.

Change the date you count from and the window moves. A day that leaves the last 180 days stops adding to the Schengen figure, but it stays in the list. The distinct-day total is the size of the whole list. The form does not know the country of a date. A typed trip is stored even when it was outside the area you meant, and a weekend you forget is simply absent.

One calendar year, and the figure 183

Year mode ignores the rolling window. It counts distinct days of the trips that fall in the chosen calendar year, and it compares the count with 183. A stay from 1 January 2026 through 2 July 2026 is 183 days: 31 in January, 28 in February, 31 in March, 30 in April, 31 in May, 30 in June, and 2 in July. A trip that crosses New Year keeps only the dates inside the chosen year. The number 183 is a figure many countries publish for a long stay. The rule that applies to a person can differ.

This is not the US substantial presence test, which weights days and looks across more than the single year on the form. This page counts a day in the chosen year as one day. It is not a tax-treaty tie-break. A treaty looks at home, family, and other facts the form never asks.

How to read the result

  • Include the day you enter and the day you leave. Both ends are dates in the list.
  • Overlapping dates stay as one day. The distinct total is the list before the window cuts it.
  • Change the count-on date to slide the 180-day window. The year field is used only in year mode.
  • A count of 90 or under is not permission to enter or to work. Stays you left out are outside the arithmetic.
  • A count of 183 is the comparison year mode prints. It is not a finding of tax residence.

Questions people ask

Do the entry day and the exit day both count?

Yes. The first date and the last date of every trip are stored. A stay that starts on Monday and ends on Wednesday is three calendar days, even if the hotel charged you for two nights. If you type only the nights and leave the departure morning out, the list is short by that date. The form will not guess a missing day. Put both ends in before you rely on the total.

What happens when two trips overlap?

The shared dates are kept once. Two rows that both include 12 March add a single 12 March, so the overlap is not double counted. The distinct-day total is the size of that set. Schengen mode then asks how many of those dates sit inside the 180-day window ending on the day you chose. Adding the same weekend twice does not push the count over 90. Leaving a trip out means those dates are absent.

Is 183 days on this page the US substantial presence test?

No. Year mode counts each distinct day in the chosen calendar year as one day and compares the total with 183. The US substantial presence test weights days and uses more than one year. This form does not apply those weights. The figure 183 is one many countries publish, and the rule for a given person can differ. The page is also not a tax-treaty tie-break. Take the day list to the rule that covers you.

Does a result under 90 mean I can enter?

It means the dates you listed are under 90 in that window, or not. Permission to enter or to work is a separate decision. The form does not see the visa in a passport or the stamps you forgot to type. On 31 March 2026 a stay from 1 January is full, at 90, and the next day of a continued stay is 91. Read the count as arithmetic, then check the rule that applies to you.

Does the day count tell me if a policy covers a routine doctor?

No. Presence days and medical cover are different questions. A nomad policy may or may not pay for a routine visit, and the answer is in the current plan, not in a day total.
