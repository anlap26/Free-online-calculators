Calorie calculator

A Mifflin–St Jeor basal rate, multiplied by an activity factor, with a rough 500 kilocalorie step on either side.

This is an equation, not a measurement of your metabolism and not a diet.

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation estimates a basal metabolic rate from weight, height, and age. In kilograms, centimetres, and years it is 10 times weight, plus 6.25 times height, minus 5 times age, and then a constant. The male constant is plus 5. The female constant is minus 161. The two forms differ by 166 kilocalories a day before activity, for the same weight, height, and age. The labels on the form name the constant, because the equation was fitted that way. It is not a judgment about anyone who does not fit the two groups in the original fit.

Pounds and inches are converted before the equation runs. One pound is 0.45359237 kilograms and one inch is 2.54 centimetres, so a body typed in either system produces the same basal figure. The activity factors are the ones commonly paired with this equation: 1.2 sedentary, 1.375 light, 1.55 moderate, 1.725 active, and 1.9 very active. Estimated daily calories are the basal figure times the factor you pick. The page also shows 500 kilocalories under that line and 500 over it. A rough rule of thumb treats about 500 kilocalories a day as half a kilogram a week. It is a sketch of a gap, not a rate your body is required to follow, and a result below zero is not a target.

A number you can recompute

Take 70 kilograms, 175 centimetres, age 30, and the male constant. The basal rate is 10×70 + 6.25×175 − 5×30 + 5, which is 700 + 1,093.75 − 150 + 5, or 1,648.75 kilocalories. Sedentary multiplies by 1.2 and gives 1,978.5. The same body with the female constant is 1,482.75, because 166 was removed. Moderate activity multiplies that by 1.55. Type the imperial equivalents, 70 divided by 0.45359237 pounds and 175 divided by 2.54 inches, and the basal rate matches. Age has to be above zero and at most 120. Weight and height have to be above zero.

The equation does not see muscle, a thyroid lab, pregnancy, or the calories in a particular meal. It does not see the pace of a run. Two people with the same inputs get the same output, which is what an equation does and what a measurement would not. If you want an index that ignores age, use BMI. If you want a circumference estimate, use the body-fat page. Neither of those multiplies by 1.2 or 1.9.

What the factors are not

  • The activity factor is a multiplier you choose, not a count of steps.
  • Five hundred kilocalories is not a prescribed deficit.
  • The equation is for adults. It is not a paediatric energy formula.
  • Switching the constant changes the basal rate by 166 for the same body size. That is the equation, not a finding about you.

BMI is on the BMI calculator. Circumferences are on the body fat calculator. Speed from a distance and a time is on the pace calculator.

Questions people ask

Which calorie equation is this?

Mifflin–St Jeor: 10×kilograms + 6.25×centimetres − 5×age, then +5 or −161. Daily calories are that result times the activity factor you pick.

What is the difference between the two constants?

166 kilocalories a day before activity, for the same weight, height, and age. The male form adds 5 and the female form subtracts 161.

What does the 500 kilocalorie line mean?

It is 500 under or over the activity-adjusted estimate. A rough sketch treats about 500 kilocalories a day as half a kilogram a week. It is not a prescription.

Do pounds and kilograms match?

Yes. Pounds are multiplied by 0.45359237 and inches by 2.54 before the equation runs.
