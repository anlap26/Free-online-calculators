Semrush free trial: what you get and how to use it

A trial is only useful if you decide, before the clock starts, which questions you will answer and which screenshots you will keep.

A Semrush trial is a short window on a paid research suite. It is not a second copy of Google Search Console, and it is not a promise that a new site will rank. The useful way to spend it is boring on purpose: one domain, one country, one topic, and a written list of what you needed to learn. People waste trials by clicking every report on the first afternoon and exporting nothing. The account then expires and the only thing left is a feeling that the tool was large.

This guide is the method, not a catalogue of the current trial. Semrush changes what a trial includes, how long it lasts, whether a card is required, and which toolkits are unlocked. Those facts belong on Semrush's own pricing page on the day you start. If a blog elsewhere quotes a seven-day trial or a particular project limit, treat that sentence as stale until you have checked. The same caution applies to any price in a comparison article, including this site's longer Semrush review and the note on Ahrefs and free tools.

What to confirm before you create the account

Read the trial terms on the page where the button is, not in a screenshot from last year. You want four answers in your own notes. First, how long the access lasts and what happens when it ends: a charge, a downgrade, or a simple stop. Second, which products are actually open. Keyword research, site audit, backlink reports, and position tracking are the modules a small site usually cares about, but a given trial may include only some of them. Third, the limits that matter in practice: how many projects, how many domains you can look up, how many reports you can run, and whether a report you ran is still visible after the trial. Fourth, the billing country and the currency, because a tax line on a card statement is not the same thing as the headline price.

Write those four answers down before you click. If the page does not say, you do not have the answer, and you should not invent one from memory. A trial that requires a card is a different commitment from a trial that does not. Cancel instructions, if you will not be staying, are part of the setup, not a task for the last hour.

Pick one site and one country

Semrush, like any rank tracker, asks which country and which language the data should use. A new site that hopes to rank in the United States and also in Germany is two projects. Mixing them in one keyword list produces a report that is true for neither market. Choose the country you can actually serve. If you only ship in English and you have no plan for a local page, do not spend the trial on a city you will never write for.

Add the domain you control. A competitor domain is useful later, as a comparison, but the first project should be yours so the audit has a real crawl target. If the site is not online yet, you can still do keyword research. You cannot audit a host that has no pages. In that case the trial is a research week, and the low-competition keyword method is the part to follow. Save the on-page work for this site's text checker, which does not need an account at all.

A first session that fits in one sitting

Start with a seed list you could explain to a friend. Ten phrases is enough. They should be things a visitor would type, not the slogan on your homepage. For this site the seeds are phrases such as compound interest calculator, Schengen 90 180, password entropy, and free meta tag generator. Yours will be different. Put the seeds into the keyword tool the trial actually opened. Expand them. You are looking for variations, questions, and phrases you did not think of, not for a score that says the work is done.

Export or copy the rows you might write. Include the phrase, the country, and whatever volume and difficulty figures the product shows, labelled with the date. Those figures are Semrush's model. They are not a search count you can take to a bank, and they will not match Ahrefs or Google Keyword Planner figure for figure. The point of saving them is so that next month you can see whether you still believe the phrase, not so that you can quote the number as a fact about the world.

Then run a site audit if the trial includes one and the site is online. Read the first page of issues, not the entire encyclopaedia. Typical early findings are pages blocked by accident, titles that repeat, a missing description, and a sitemap that does not match the pages you care about. You can draft the files themselves in the robots and sitemap generator and the titles in the meta tag generator. Semrush is telling you the crawl saw a problem. It is not publishing the fix.

What to record before the window closes

Assume you cannot log back in. That assumption is what makes the trial pay for itself even if you do not subscribe. Keep a short document with five things. The keyword list you would actually write, with the country and the date. Three competitors you looked at and one sentence on why each page ranks, in your own words, not a pasted metric. The audit issues you accept as real, each with the URL. The tracking keywords you would measure later, a dozen at most. And a sentence on what the trial did not include, so you do not remember a locked module as if you had tested it.

Screenshots help if the export button was limited. Name the files with the date. A screenshot without the country and the tool name is not a note you can use in six weeks. Position tracking, if you started it, may not have a trend yet. A tracker needs time. Do not judge a new project by the first day's line.

How the free tools on this site fit around the trial

The trial is for questions that need an index: who else ranks, how hard the product thinks the phrase is, what the crawl found across the whole domain. Several jobs do not need that index. Counting words and keyword density in a draft is the text analyzer. Writing the title, the description, and the Open Graph tags is the meta generator. A robots file and a sitemap you can paste onto the host come from the robots and sitemap tool. Campaign links and a QR code are the UTM builder. None of those pages send your draft to Semrush. Use them before the trial so you arrive with a real page, and after the trial so the subscription is not required to fix a title.

If you are also paying for model tokens to draft those pages, the token calculator is a separate bill. A cheap draft that targets a phrase nobody searches is still a waste. The trial is where you check the phrase. The token tool is where you check the invoice. Do not add those two costs together and call the sum an SEO budget until you have decided you will publish.

What a trial will not settle

It will not tell you that you should buy the subscription. That depends on whether you will open it next month. A site you update once a year does not need a tracker. A site you publish on every week often does, because the alternative is guessing which phrases to write. It will not measure your server. Page speed is a different question. The revenue sketch on this site uses the seconds and the conversion you type. It is not a Semrush report, and a Semrush audit is not a hosting benchmark. Hosts are compared in the Cloudways and Kinsta note, with the same refusal to invent a timing number.

It will not write the page. A keyword list without a page that answers the query is a spreadsheet. It will not fix a title that promises something the page does not contain. And it will not show your own clicks. Those live in Google Search Console, which remains worth connecting whether or not you keep Semrush. Search Console is what your site actually received. Semrush is a model of the wider market. You want both, and you do not want to pretend they are the same report.

If you stay, and if you do not

If you subscribe, shrink the project to the reports you used in the trial. More toolkits feel like progress and often mean you stop opening the account. If you do not subscribe, you still have the notes, the drafts, and the free checks on this site. You can run another trial later only if the terms allow it. Do not create a stack of accounts to dodge a limit. Read the current rules.

Questions people ask

How long is the Semrush free trial?

This page does not print a number of days. Semrush changes the length, the card requirement, and the modules. Read the pricing page on the day you start, and write the answer in your own notes before you rely on it.

What should I do on the first day?

One domain, one country, a short seed list, an export of the phrases you might write, and the first page of a site audit if the site is online. Do not try to open every report.

Will the trial show my Google clicks?

No. Clicks and queries your site already receives are in Google Search Console. Semrush models a wider market. The two reports answer different questions.

Do I need Semrush to use the calculators here?

No. The text checker, meta tags, robots file, and UTM builder run in the browser. The trial is for index questions those pages cannot see.

What should I save before the trial ends?

The keyword list with the country and the date, a few competitor notes in your own words, the audit issues you accept, and a sentence on which modules were locked. Assume you cannot log back in.
