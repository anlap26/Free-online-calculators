Binance and Bybit: residency, fees, and where the coins sit

Two large exchanges compared on the restrictions, the fee cell, and the difference between a balance on the venue and coins you hold.

Binance and Bybit are two large crypto exchanges. People compare them when they want a place to buy, sell, and sometimes to park coins in a yield product. An exchange is a company with an order book, a custody operation, and a list of countries it will not serve. It is not a wallet you hold, and it is not advice to trade. This article compares the questions a resident of somewhere specific has to ask. It does not rank the two firms, does not quote a volume, and does not narrate a hack. Those numbers and those histories go stale or get argued, and this page will not invent them.

The first question is whether either firm will take you. Both restrict residency. The list changes. Your passport and the country where you live can both matter, and they can matter differently. Read the current restricted-country page and the questions on the application. If you are excluded, you are excluded. A VPN does not make you an eligible customer. Using one to appear to live somewhere else can break the terms and the law where you actually are. The VPN guide is about tunnels on networks you do not trust. It is not a costume for a signup form.

How to compare without a scoreboard

Put the two fee schedules next to each other and read the lines you will actually use. Spot trading, a derivatives contract, and a conversion from fiat are different schedules. Maker and taker are different. A VIP tier, a token discount, and a promotion are different again, and they expire. Write down the tier the account would really be on, not the tier in the banner. Then type that fee into the trading fee calculator. The calculator adds a commission and slippage you type, and it solves a sell quote that gets back to flat. It does not know this week's Binance or Bybit schedule. If you cannot point at the cell you typed, you are not comparing fees. You are comparing memory.

Do the same for the product list. A coin listed on one venue and not the other ends the comparison for that coin. A derivatives contract with a funding payment is not the same instrument as the coin. Read the contract specification: what the funding does, how liquidation works, and what happens to open positions if the market is halted. This page will not walk a leverage trade. Leverage can remove more than the sum you thought you risked. If you use it anyway, the position size calculator turns a stop distance and a percent of the account into a quantity. It does not tell you to take the trade.

Binance

Binance is the exchange people mean when they say the large global venue, and it is also a set of regional entities. Which entity would hold your account depends on where you live. The international site and a local regulated affiliate are not interchangeable. Read the legal name on the terms you are asked to accept. A feature visible in a screenshot from another country may be absent, renamed, or forbidden on yours. Fiat deposits, in particular, follow local payment rules. A card that works for a friend can fail for you, and the failure is not a review of the matching engine.

Fee tables on Binance have long been something a user must open, because discounts and VIP levels change the number. Open today's table. Earn products and staking-style products sit beside the exchange and change faster than the spot fee. A quoted rate is an advertisement until the page states the term, whether the coins are locked, who the counterparty is, and whether you are lending the coins onward. Flexible and locked are different risks. A reward paid in a token the platform itself influences is a different reward from a fee rebate. Read the current product. Do not inherit an annual percentage from a video.

Bybit

Bybit is the other name on this shortlist, used heavily by people who came for derivatives and stayed for spot and for earn-style products. The same residency test applies, and it will not have the same answers as Binance. A country that one firm serves may be refused by the other this year and reversed next year. Check both lists on the day you apply. The fee schedule is likewise its own document: maker, taker, a derivatives fee that is not the spot fee, and any discount that depends on holding a token or hitting a volume. Volume tiers are a promise about a future you may not reach. Price the tier you are in.

Earn and staking products on Bybit, as on any large venue, can change without the comparison article changing. Treat a high advertised rate as a prompt to read the lockup, the cap on how much you can place, and what the firm says happens if it suspends withdrawals. The staking calculator compounds a rate you type and shows an effective yearly figure from that assumption. It is not Bybit's rate and not Binance's rate. If the product pays out on a schedule that is not simple compounding, the calculator will not reproduce the product. It will tell you what the rate you typed would do if it compounded the way the form says. That is still worth knowing before you trust a banner.

Custody, and savings that should leave

Coins on an exchange are a claim on the company. Coins in a wallet whose keys you hold are coins you can move without the company's permission. Those are different arrangements. An exchange can freeze a sub-account, pause withdrawals, lose a court case, or lose coins it was holding. This article does not date those events and does not claim one firm has a cleaner past than the other. It says the risk is structural. A trading balance that must sit on the venue so you can act is a trading balance. Savings you would be unhappy to wait for are a bad fit for the same login.

For savings that should not sit on the exchange, read the hardware wallet guide. The device signs with a key that stays on the device. The recovery phrase is the real secret. Moving coins off the venue has its own fee and its own chance of a wrong address. Send a small test if the network makes that possible, and read the current withdrawal page for the chain you are actually using. A token on the wrong network is a different loss from a hack, and it is a loss the wallet cannot undo.

Proof of reserves is a claim

Both firms, and others, have at times published proof-of-reserves material. Treat it as a claim to read, not as a comfort to repeat. A reserve report, when it exists, is a snapshot with a method. Read who signed it, which assets were included, which liabilities were matched to customer balances, and what was left out: loans, related parties, assets off the venue, accounts under a different entity. A report is not the same thing as a bank audit under a regime you already understand. A report that a reviewer could not re-run is a PDF, not a proof. The absence of a score in this article is deliberate. If you cannot explain the method in a few sentences, you have not finished reading it.

Fills, averages, and the calculators

If you bought in several fills, the average that includes fees is the number that matters for a break-even. The venue's own screen may treat fees differently from the way you think about them. The average price calculator takes the quantities, the prices, and the fees you type and returns one average and a break-even sale price. Use your own fills. It will not log into either exchange. Together with the fee calculator, it is how this site expects a reader to touch numbers: from the schedule and the statement in front of them, not from a table this page refuses to invent.

The order book the calculator cannot see

The fee calculator does not see an order book. The slippage you type is your estimate of how far a fill moves from the price on the screen. On a thin pair that estimate matters more than the commission. On a deep pair the commission can matter more. Neither Binance nor Bybit fills the form with its own depth. To compare one trade on both venues, use the same price, the same size, and the two fees from the two schedules. The gap you get is a gap in fees under your assumption, not a measurement of liquidity.

Who it fits

Either exchange fits a person who has checked they are allowed to open the account, who will read the fee cell they actually pay, and who will not leave long-term savings on the venue. Choosing between Binance and Bybit is then mostly residency, the markets and contracts you will really use, the fiat path you can lawfully use, and the schedule on the screen. This page does not know which of those favours you. It is a weak fit, for both, if you wanted a savings account with a government guarantee. Crypto on an exchange is not that product. A person who only needed a price should not need either account.

The one whose current schedule, at the tier your account would really be on, charges less for the product you will actually use. Spot, derivatives, and fiat conversion are different tables. They are a claim on the company. Coins in a wallet whose keys you hold can move without the company's permission. An exchange can pause withdrawals or freeze an account.
