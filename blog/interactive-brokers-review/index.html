Interactive Brokers when you do not live in one market

What people look up this broker for, which documents depend on your residency, and why the compound calculator is not a past return.

Some links on this page are affiliate links. If you open an account or buy through them, we may earn a commission. You do not pay more because of that. The calculator does not change its result to suit a partner. Affiliate disclosure

Interactive Brokers is the name people look up when they invest from a country that is not the only market they care about. The group is known for access to many exchanges and for accounts that can hold more than one currency. That is the category. It is not a reason to buy any asset, and this article does not contain a past return, a forecast, or a list of funds. A broker is a place an order can be sent and a place cash can sit between orders. The arithmetic of a long savings plan lives in the contribution and the rate you assume. The logo on the account does not supply either.

The group operates through different legal entities. The one named on your account agreement depends on where you live. A product page written for another country is not your product page. Before you compare fees with a neighbour, read the legal name on the agreement you were offered. Two customers can say they use Interactive Brokers and be customers of different entities, with different forms, different market permissions, and different fee schedules. Check the current schedule for your entity. Numbers move, and this page will not quote one.

How to check it for your residency

Start with eligibility. The application asks where you live and, often, your citizenship. If the firm does not accept that combination, the rest of this article is a description of a product you cannot open. Do not treat a VPN, a friend's address, or a company formed in a hurry as a way through that door. A false answer on an account application is a problem this page will not help you create.

Then read which markets your entity will actually let you trade. A brand that advertises a long list of exchanges is advertising the group's reach. Your login may show a shorter list. Stocks, bonds, funds, options, futures, and currency conversion are different permissions. A market that appears in a commercial can be missing from the account, or present only as a product you do not want, such as a derivative where you thought you were buying the share. Open the permission screen after the account exists, and read it against the markets you named before you applied.

Tax forms are the next document, and they are not tax advice. Brokers ask non-residents and residents different questions. You may be asked to certify where you are taxed. The firm may later produce a form that summarises income it paid you. You still file what your own country requires, including income the form does not show. This page does not know your treaty, your brackets, or whether a particular fund reports in a way your accountant can use. Read what the broker asks, keep what the broker sends, and take the actual question to someone who is allowed to answer it.

Currency, conversion, and fees that change

Multi-currency is the feature that makes the account interesting for a person paid in one currency who buys assets in another. It is also the feature that hides a cost. A conversion can be a separate order you place, or an automatic conversion the platform does when you buy. The commission, the spread, and the moment the rate is set are on the current fee schedule and in the preview of the order. Read both. A schedule from a forum is not the schedule for your entity this month.

Inactivity fees and account fees belong in the same reading. They have changed before, and they differ by entity and by balance. This article will not quote them. Look for a monthly minimum, a fee that is waived if you generate commissions, and a fee for closing or for sending money out. A waived fee is still a rule. If your plan is to contribute twice a year and otherwise leave the account alone, the inactivity line matters more than the headline commission on a share you will rarely trade. Market data is often a separate subscription. A price on a screen can be delayed unless you pay, and a delayed price is a bad thing to size a trade from.

What the planning calculators are for

The compound interest calculator is the planning maths. You type a starting sum, an annual rate, a period, and an optional monthly contribution. It compounds that assumption. It does not know what markets will do, and it does not know which broker holds the cash. Interactive Brokers is one place the contributions might sit. A bank deposit, a pension, or another broker are other places. The same formula applies. If you want to see the path year by year, the table is there. The rate you type is yours. Past returns are not supplied on this page, because a past return is not an input you should quietly inherit.

The FIRE calculator turns yearly spending and a withdrawal rate you choose into a portfolio number. Add a contribution and an assumed return and it sketches a time to reach that number. The sketch breaks if the return is fantasy or if spending is incomplete. It does not say that this broker, or any broker, will deliver the return. It does not include tax. Use it to see how large a number your spending implies, then decide separately where the contributions live.

The position size calculator is for a person who already has an entry, a stop, and a sum they can afford to lose. It converts a percent of the account and a stop distance into a quantity. It is not a suggestion to trade, and it is not a view on a ticker. The broker will not set your risk for you. If you do not use stops, you do not need the tool, and you still need a rule for how much of the account one idea can consume. The currency spread calculator compares an amount a bank delivers with the amount an interbank rate you type would have delivered. That is a way to see a gap in a transfer. The broker's own FX conversion is a different quote. Read it on their schedule and, if you want, type those figures into the same form. The form does not fetch them.

Statements, cash, and what the account will not do for you

Once the account exists, the statement is the document that matters more than the dashboard. Read which currency each line is in, whether a conversion happened without a separate order, and whether a fee was netted against a dividend so the dividend looks smaller than the announcement. Withholding on a foreign dividend is a tax event the broker may apply because the listing country requires it. It is not a judgement on the investment. It is a reason to know, before you buy a market you have never held, which form will show that withholding and which form will not. This page cannot tell you whether your country gives a credit for it.

Moving cash in and out is its own checklist. The name on the sending account, the currencies the broker will receive, and the time a withdrawal takes are operational facts on the current help page. A multi-currency balance does not by itself move money to your landlord. Someone still has to send a transfer, and someone still has to compare the broker's conversion with a bank's conversion. Use the spread calculator for that comparison when you have both quotes. Do not assume the broker is cheaper because the interface is more serious. Serious is not a price.

The account will not decide an allocation, rebalance a portfolio, or notice that your spending in the FIRE sketch left out rent. Those are your inputs. It will also not hold a private key for a coin you withdrew, if a crypto product is even offered to your entity. If it is offered, read whether you hold a coin or a derivative, and read the custody sentence. For coins you intend to keep outside any exchange or broker, the hardware-wallet article is the relevant one. Funding a brokerage account and calling it a vault mixes two kinds of promise.

Account type and order type

Before you fund the account, name the type. A cash account buys with money already there. A margin account can lend the rest, which changes how fast a position can be closed for you. If you do not want leverage, check that the application does not turn it on with a tick. This page does not calculate a margin requirement. A market order accepts the price you are given. A limit names the worst price you accept. Neither is advice. If the screen defaults to a market order, open the current help. A default is not a virtue.

Who it fits

Interactive Brokers fits an investor who needs more than one market or more than one currency and who will read a dense schedule and a legal entity name. It fits someone paid in one currency who buys in another and wants the conversion to be a line they can inspect. It is a weak fit if you want a single domestic app for one stock market and you will not open a fee PDF. It is no fit at all if your residency is on the list the firm refuses. The review cannot route around that list.

It is also the wrong article if the question is coins you hold yourself. A brokerage balance is a claim on the firm, under the firm's rules and the firm's regulators. A hardware wallet is a different arrangement, described in the wallet guide. Mixing the two because both involve money is how people leave long-term savings in a product built for trading, or buy a device for assets a broker already holds in a regulated account. Pick the arrangement first. Then pick the firm.

This site did not measure execution quality, borrow rates, margin interest, platform outages, or how long support takes in your language. It did not check tax-lot reports against a return. It did not open accounts in a sample of countries to see which markets appear. Those are tests a customer can do after approval, with the permission screen and the statement in hand. Publishing a fee here would make the page wrong on the day the schedule changes. An affiliate link can pay a commission. It does not add a market your entity withholds.

Currency conversion has a cost even when the screen looks like a convenience. Tax remains yours.
