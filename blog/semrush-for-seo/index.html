Semrush for a small site, without the drown

Keyword research, a site audit, backlinks, and position tracking, used as one country, one list, and one crawl. No price is quoted, and no audit was run here.

Semrush is a research suite people open when they want to know which phrases a page might be found for, what a crawl thinks is broken, who links to a site, and whether a page's position moved. Those are four jobs: keyword research, a site audit, backlinks, and position tracking. A small site does not have to do all four on the first afternoon, and it should not. The way to use a large suite without drowning is to pick one country, one set of queries, and one audit, and to finish them before you collect a second report. This page describes that discipline. It does not quote a price, because plans change, and it does not claim that we ran an audit of anyone's site.

Other research suites exist. This page does not compare them. A reader who already pays for a different one can keep the same limits: one market, one list, one crawl. Google Search Console remains the free source of queries that already produced impressions on your own site. A third-party suite estimates a wider corpus. The console shows what you received. The suite is where you look for phrases you do not rank for yet. They are different documents. Neither is a promise that a new paragraph will be placed anywhere.

One country, one query set, one audit

Start with the country you actually serve. A global database will suggest phrases nobody in your market types, in a language you do not publish, with a volume figure that belongs to somewhere else. Set the country and the language in the tool and leave them there for the month. If you serve two countries, run two short lists, not one blended list that you cannot act on.

One query set means a list you can finish. Take the phrases already on the page, the phrases a reader would type, and the phrases a competitor's title obviously targets. Look each one up and write down what the tool reports: whether it thinks anyone searches, how it groups related phrases, and which URL of yours, if any, it associates with the phrase. Volumes in this kind of tool are the vendor's estimates, not a census. Treat them as directional. A phrase with no estimated demand is a weak reason to rewrite a page, unless your own Search Console already shows impressions for it. A phrase with demand is still a weak reason if you cannot say what the page will answer.

One audit means you fix the issues you understand before you order another crawl. A missing title, a canonical that points at the wrong place, a noindex you did not mean, a sitemap the tool cannot fetch: those are decisions. A list of two hundred warnings is not a decision. Export the ones you recognise, fix them, and crawl again later. Semrush will still be there. Position tracking belongs on the short list, not on every phrase the database offered. A handful of queries, checked in the country you chose, tells you whether the page moved. Hundreds of queries on day one produce a dashboard.

What the local text tool can and cannot see

The text analyzer on this site counts words, characters, sentences, and a keyword density. Density is the number of hits, times the words in the phrase, divided by all the words. It runs in the browser. It does not know whether anyone searches the phrase, and it does not know whether the phrase is already in your title. Use it to see that you have repeated a string until the page sounds broken, or that a phrase you care about never appears. Then use Semrush, or Search Console, to ask the demand question the local count cannot ask. A high density is not a ranking method. It is a reason to rewrite for a human.

The meta tag generator writes a title, a description, and the social tags that go with them, and it warns when the title or the description is long enough to be cut in a result. Truncation is not a ranking. It is a reason to put the useful words where they will still be visible. Write the title after you know the phrase, not before. The generator does not look up the phrase. You bring the phrase from the research suite or from the console.

Files, campaigns, and a cost that is not ranking

An audit often says a sitemap is missing or that a robots file blocks something you wanted crawled. The robots and sitemap generator drafts those files from paths you list. You still upload them to the host, and you still check that the live file matches what you meant. A draft is not a deployment. The hosting comparison is about where the file lives, not about whether the phrase has demand.

The UTM and QR tool builds a tagged link and a code for a campaign you run: a newsletter, a poster, a partner. Those clicks are not organic positions. Do not paste a tagged URL into a keyword report and call it a ranking. Tag the campaigns you need to tell apart, and leave ordinary links untagged. A QR code does not change the page's content. It only carries the address.

The token calculator is a different bill. It multiplies tokens by prices you can edit. The starter prices are placeholders in a file, not a quote from a model vendor. Paying a model to draft a paragraph does not place the page. The cost of the draft and the question of whether anyone searches the phrase are separate. A cheap draft of a phrase nobody types is still a page nobody asked for. A careful page about a phrase your console already shows can be written without a model at all. If you do use one, put the real price in the calculator and keep that number out of the ranking conversation.

Backlinks without a project you will not finish

Backlink reports are where suites get large. A small site needs a short reading, not a campaign. Look at the links the tool already associates with your domain and ask which of them you recognise. Then look at a competitor only far enough to see the kind of page that attracts links you could honestly earn: a reference, a data table you will maintain, a guide you will update. This article does not teach a scheme for buying links or for placing them where they were not asked for. A link you cannot explain to the person who owns the other site is a bad use of the report. Record the few links that matter, in a note you will still understand in three months. The report itself will have changed by then.

A month that stays small

A workable month looks like this. In the first sitting, set the country and write the query list from the page you already have, not from a brainstorm of fifty ideas. Look up that list. Mark the phrases the tool thinks have demand and the phrases your Search Console already shows. Keep both marks. They will not match, and the mismatch is information: demand you do not receive yet, and impressions the suite did not predict.

In the second sitting, run one audit and fix only the rows you can explain to a colleague without the tool open. A broken canonical, a blocked page, a title that does not match the phrase you just chose. Leave the cosmetic warnings. In the third sitting, turn tracking on for the short list and do not add to it. For the rest of the month, write or revise the one page the list pointed at. Backlinks, if you open them at all, are a fourth sitting with a notebook, not a second product. This schedule is a way to stop the suite expanding the work. It is not a claim that Semrush recommends it, and it is not a result we measured on a client site.

Reading a position without a story

A tracking row is a phrase, a country, a URL, and a position that moved or did not. Read it as that. A drop after you changed a title is a reason to look at the title, not a reason to buy another module. A rise after you changed nothing is a reason to be suspicious of a one-week sample. Positions move. The useful comparison is the same phrase, the same country, the same URL, across more than one check. The tool can show that series. It cannot tell you that the series will continue. Pair it with Search Console's own impression and click counts for the same phrase, and you have two partial instruments instead of one dashboard you have to believe.

Do not open a second URL yet

A common extra job starts with a phrase the site already has a page for. The suite may offer a topic that is only a different word order of your title. Before a new address, see which URL already gets impressions for that family in Search Console. Two addresses for one job compete. Editing the page you have is smaller, and easier to finish inside the month you limited. Mark queries that use your name apart from the rest. A high position on the name can sit beside a weak generic phrase. A short list split that way is more honest.

Who it fits

Semrush fits a small site that will pay for one research suite and then refuse to open every module. It fits an editor who can keep a query list on a page and an audit list in a ticket. It is a weak fit if you wanted a machine to write the site, or if the only questions you have are already answered by Search Console and by reading your own titles. A free account or a trial may exist. Confirm that on the current page, and confirm the price of the plan you would keep. Do not inherit either from this article.

What this site did not measure

This site did not run a Semrush audit, did not export a keyword list, and did not test how close the volume estimates are to Search Console on any sample. It did not time support or count a database. Accuracy stories belong to a study this page is not. An affiliate link can pay a commission. It does not add demand for a phrase, and it does not make a density figure into a ranking. If the product's own page disagrees with a feature described here, the product page wins.

Limits

Plans and prices change. Check the current page before you subscribe. Data in a research suite is the vendor's estimate and can be wrong in your country even when the country filter is set. Rankings move for reasons that have nothing to do with a tool: a better page, a changed index, a query nobody repeats. Search Console stays the record of your own impressions. The text analyzer stays a local count. The token calculator stays a bill for a model. None of them is Semrush, and Semrush is not a substitute for a page that answers something.

Questions people ask

Do I need every Semrush module on day one?

No. Pick the country you serve, one list of queries you can finish, and one audit. Fix the rows you understand, such as a missing title, a canonical that points at the wrong place, or a noindex you did not mean. Turn position tracking on for that short list only. A second report before the first is finished is how a small site drowns. Other suites exist. This page does not compare them. The same limits apply if you already pay for a different one.

Is keyword density a ranking score?

No. The text analyzer on this site counts density locally: hits, times the words in the phrase, divided by all words. It does not know whether anyone searches. Use it to see that a page repeats a string until it sounds broken, or that a phrase never appears. Use Semrush or Search Console for the demand question. Volumes in a research suite are the vendor's estimates, not a census. A high density is a reason to rewrite for a person, not a method.

Where do my real queries come from for free?

Google Search Console shows queries that already produced impressions on your own site. A suite such as Semrush estimates a wider corpus, including phrases you do not rank for yet. Keep both. They will not match, and the mismatch is the point: demand you do not receive, and impressions the suite did not predict. This article does not speak for Google, and the console's screens change. Export the queries you will actually write a page for, and leave the rest.

Does paying for an AI draft help the page rank?

The token calculator multiplies tokens by prices you edit. Those starter prices are placeholders, not a quote. The bill for a draft is separate from whether anyone searches the phrase. A cheap draft of a phrase nobody types is still a page nobody asked for. A page about a phrase your console already shows can be written with no model at all. If you use one, keep that cost out of the ranking conversation. Semrush does not become a writer because a model is cheap.

Did this site run a Semrush audit?

No. We did not crawl a sample site, did not export a keyword list, and did not test volume estimates against Search Console. Prices are not quoted because they change. Confirm the current plan, including whether a trial exists, before you subscribe. An affiliate link can pay a commission. It does not add demand for a phrase. If the product page disagrees with a feature described here, believe the product page. Rankings also move for reasons that have nothing to do with the tool.
