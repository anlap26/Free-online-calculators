How to find low-competition keywords for a new site with Semrush

A new site does not beat a crowded head term by wanting it. It looks for a phrase it can answer clearly, then checks that the results page is actually beatable.

Low competition is not a trophy and not a filter you can set once. It is a judgement: for this phrase, in this country, the pages already ranking are something you can match or beat with a page you are willing to write. A difficulty number is one input to that judgement. It is a model maintained by Semrush. It is not a measurement of how hard the work will feel on a Tuesday, and it is not a prediction that you will rank. A new site that sorts the entire language by "easiest" and publishes the top fifty will mostly produce pages nobody needed.

This is a method for the first months of a site, when you have little authority and you cannot afford to spend a month on a head term owned by manuals, marketplaces, and brands. It assumes you will open Semrush, or a trial of it, as described in the trial guide. If you are still deciding whether Semrush is the suite at all, read Semrush, Ahrefs, and free tools first. The buttons and report names below are the kind of report to look for. Semrush renames screens. If a label is gone, look for the job, not for the old name.

Start from a job, not from a score

Write the jobs a visitor has. For a calculator site the jobs are concrete: find the future value of a monthly contribution, count Schengen days, see if a colour pair passes WCAG, price an API call. Each job suggests seeds a person would type. "Calculator" alone is not a seed you can win. "Compound interest calculator with monthly contributions" is closer to a page you can actually build, because you already know the formula and the worked example.

Put a short seed list into keyword research. Fix the country and the language before you look at any number. A phrase that is quiet in one country can be crowded in another. Export the variations, the questions, and the phrases Semrush groups near the seed. You are collecting candidates, not choosing yet. Keep the phrase, the country, and the date on every row you keep. A number without those three is not a note.

Read the results page before you trust the difficulty

Sort is a convenience. It is not the decision. Take the candidates you could write this month and open a real search for them, in the same country, in a window where you are not wildly personalised if you can help it. Read the first page. Ask four questions. Are the results pages that answer the same job, or are they dictionaries, videos, and product grids? Could you publish something a reader would finish? Do you already have the facts, or would you be inventing them? Is the intent something you want on a site that also has to be maintained?

A low difficulty score on a phrase whose results are a government manual and two national newspapers is not low competition for you. A higher score on a phrase whose results are thin, outdated, or aimed at a different reader can be the better page. The score did not see your constraints. You did. Write one sentence on why you believe you can be the better result. If you cannot write the sentence, drop the phrase. The Semrush review makes the same point in the other direction: the suite shows a market, it does not owe you a ranking.

Prefer phrases that match a page you can finish

A new site wins by being the obvious answer to a narrow question. Modifiers do that work. A year, a place, a file format, a comparison of two named things, a formula with its inputs. "Best VPN" is a market. "Does a web page show my DNS resolver" is a question this site can answer without pretending to run a lab, which is why the IP tool's copy is careful. Match that standard. If the honest answer is "it depends, and here is the formula", that is a page. If the honest answer is a price you would have to invent, it is not a page.

Questions are useful when you will answer them, and useless when you will only repeat the question in a heading. Semrush's question-style rows are a list of prompts. Your page still needs the answer in the first screen. The text analyzer then tells you whether the phrase you chose actually appears, and whether you have repeated it so often that the draft sounds broken. Density is a diagnostic. It is not a ranking lever you can stuff.

A working set, not a warehouse

Stop at a set you can publish. For a new site that is often ten to twenty phrases, each tied to a URL you have not built yet. More than that becomes a graveyard of ideas. Give each phrase one page. Give each page one job. A page that tries to rank for the head term and three unrelated questions will be vague, and vague pages are how new sites look like everyone else.

Write the title and the description only after the phrase is chosen. The meta tag generator will warn you when the title is long enough to be cut. It will not pick the phrase. Put the phrase where a reader can see it, in words you would say out loud. Then draft. If a model is writing the first pass, put the tokens through the token calculator so the drafting cost is visible. A page that costs more to generate than it can ever return is a hobby, which is allowed, but it should not be confused with a plan.

What to ignore in the first month

Ignore huge volume on phrases you cannot answer. Ignore difficulty comparisons across countries. Ignore a backlink gap on a site that has no pages yet. Ignore the urge to track five hundred phrases. A dozen tracked phrases, in one country, is a report you will read. Five hundred is a report you will not.

Ignore anyone who sells a list of "zero competition" phrases with no results page attached. If the results are empty because nobody wants the phrase, you have found a quiet street with no visitors. Low competition is only interesting when there is a reader. Volume models are flawed, but a phrase with no one searching and no one ranking is not a secret opportunity. It is often just silence.

After the page exists

Publish, add the URL to the sitemap you generate, and request indexing in Search Console rather than waiting for a suite to notice. Semrush can track the phrase afterwards. It cannot insert you into the index. Give the page a chance to be crawled before you rewrite it because a rank tracker showed nothing on day two. Trackers lag. Consoles lag. Rewriting daily teaches you nothing.

When you do revisit, compare the page to the results you saved, not to a new score you sorted. Did you answer the job? Is the title still true? Did you drift into a broader phrase you cannot win? The speed sketch matters only if the page is slow enough to be the problem. A fast page about the wrong phrase is still the wrong page.

One more filter belongs on the list before you export. Group the candidates by the page you would actually ship, not by the score. If three phrases would be answered by the same screen, they are one page with a primary phrase and two sentences of support, not three URLs. Splitting them into three thin pages is how a new site creates duplicates of itself. Semrush will happily show all three rows. The decision to merge them is yours, and it should happen in the spreadsheet, with a column called URL, before any draft starts.

Open Semrush when you are ready to run the export. The link is a partner link. The method works only if the phrases you keep are ones you can finish. A lower score is not a publishing schedule.

Questions people ask

What keyword difficulty is low enough?

There is no universal cutoff. The number is Semrush's model for one country. Open the results and decide whether you can answer the job better than the pages already there.

Should a new site chase high-volume terms?

Not as the first bet. Head terms are often owned by manuals, brands, and marketplaces. Start with a phrase you can answer completely on one page.

How many phrases should I track?

A dozen in one country is a report you will read. Hundreds become a list you ignore. Publish the pages before you expand the tracker.

Does keyword density find the phrase?

No. Density checks a draft after you have chosen the phrase. It does not know whether anyone searches it. That question is the index, which is the suite.

Can I do this without Semrush?

You can read the results page and write the draft with the free tools here. You cannot see Semrush's volume and difficulty models without Semrush or a similar index. Do not mix two products' scores.
