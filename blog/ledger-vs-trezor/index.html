Ledger and Trezor: the device, the phrase, and the backup

A hardware wallet signs on the device. The recovery phrase is the secret. Here is what to read before choosing Ledger or Trezor, including the 2023 recovery debate.

A hardware wallet is a small device whose job is to hold a private key and to sign a transaction where you can see it. The computer or the phone builds the transaction and asks the device to sign. The key is supposed to stay on the device during ordinary use. The signature goes back. The coins themselves do not live inside the device. They live on the network. The device is a way to approve a move without putting the key in a browser. That is the whole category. Ledger and Trezor are two makers people compare inside it. This page says what each design is associated with, and what you still have to check on the current spec. It does not rank them, and it does not quote a price.

The recovery phrase is the real secret. Devices commonly produce a list of words, often twelve or twenty-four, from which the keys can be rebuilt. Anyone who has the phrase has the coins. The device is replaceable. A lost phrase, or a phrase that someone else photographed, is not. Write the words down the way the device tells you to, on something that is not a screenshot, not a cloud note, and not an email to yourself. Check the words before you send any serious amount, while the pain of a mistake is still small. Neither Ledger nor Trezor can protect a phrase you stored in a note that syncs.

How to compare without a lab

You do not need a lab that drills chips. You need the current spec sheet, the current explanation of the backup, and a decision you can say out loud: will you ever turn on a feature that can reconstruct the key from pieces held elsewhere. Read whether the firmware is open, whether a secure element is part of the model you are buying, and whether the screen shows the address and the amount. A review that says one brand is safer in general is skipping the model number. Newer models differ from older ones. Buy the model whose sheet you read, from the maker's own shop if you can. A pre-initialized device from a marketplace is a known way to lose coins, because someone else may already have the phrase. That warning is old and it still applies to both brands.

Ledger

Ledger is a hardware-wallet maker. Its devices use a secure element, a chip designed to resist someone who has the device in their hands and time to work on it. Confirm that the model you buy still matches that description. The firmware is not fully open. Parts of the stack and some apps have been published at times. The full firmware has not been something a stranger can read line by line. You are trusting the vendor and the chip more than you are auditing every instruction. Read what source they publish now, and read the date. Closed firmware is a real objection for some people and an acceptable trade for others. Name which you are.

In 2023 Ledger discussed Recover, an optional paid design to split a backup so a recovery service could help restore a key after identity checks. It was widely criticised. The objection was not that a paid option existed in the abstract. The objection was that a path for key material to leave the device, even an optional one, changed what people thought the device had promised. Whether you would enable anything like it is a decision to make before you buy, not after a prompt on a Tuesday. Check what the product does now. The feature may have been renamed, limited, or left available in a form you still refuse. A criticism from 2023 does not describe the checkbox in front of you. The checkbox does not erase the criticism. Read both.

Trezor

Trezor is associated with open-source firmware. The point of that association is that the code which asks the chip to sign can be read, built, and compared with the device by people who are not the vendor. Open firmware is not a force field. It means defects can be looked for in public, and it means you still depend on the hardware, the supply chain, and your own handling of the phrase. A bug in open code is still a bug. The advantage is that the bug is not also a secret.

Do not assume every Trezor lacks a secure element. Newer models may differ. Read the current spec of the model you are about to pay for, rather than a forum post about an older one. A model with a secure element and published firmware is a different product from an older model with neither, or from a Ledger device with a secure element and closed firmware. The labels "open" and "secure element" are not a single axis with a winner at one end. Write down what the sheet of your model actually says, then decide whether that combination matches the threat you have. A pickpocket and a sophisticated physical attacker are not the same threat. A cloud note is a third threat, and neither chip helps with it.

The phrase, the passphrase, and the tools that must not touch it

Both ecosystems have offered an optional extra passphrase on top of the word list. It creates a different wallet. If you forget it, the coins are gone. The vendor cannot reset it. People sometimes call it a twenty-fifth word. Check the current name and the current warning before you rely on the habit. It is a good feature for a person who will remember an extra secret or store it as carefully as the phrase. It is a bad feature to turn on because a video said to, and then to keep only in your head.

Do not type a hardware-wallet phrase into any website, including this one. The password generator is for passwords and for passphrases you choose to create for accounts. The seed on the device is generated by the device. The generator can help you make a long PIN or a separate passphrase if you want randomness you can read, and you should then write that extra secret down offline. It must not be where you "generate" or "check" the seed words. The AES encryptor locks a note in the browser with a password that never leaves the machine. Lose the password and the note is gone. Encrypting a seed into a file and uploading the file is a ceremony this article will not teach, because a cloud copy of the only secret is the failure the device cannot see. Use the encryptor to understand local encryption. Use paper, or another offline backup you understand, for the phrase.

Inflation is a different problem

Protecting coins from a custodian is a different problem from the currency losing buying power. A hardware wallet answers the first. It does not answer the second. A coin that nobody else can move can still buy less food next year. The inflation calculator takes a rate you type and shows what the same basket costs later and what today's sum still buys. The rate is your assumption, not a forecast and not a reason to hold any asset. People sometimes buy a hardware wallet because they are worried about prices in the shop, and then leave the phrase in a screenshot because the shop felt like the urgent problem. The wallet did not fail. The backup did. Run the inflation arithmetic if you want to see purchasing power. Run the backup check if you want the coins to still be yours. They do not substitute for each other.

An exchange balance, described in the exchange comparison, is the opposite custody choice: convenient for a trade, dependent on the company for a withdrawal. A hardware wallet is the choice for an amount you can stand to move slowly and carefully. Many people keep a small trading balance on a venue and a larger amount on a device. That split is a habit, not a rule this page measured. The habit fails if the larger amount's phrase is in the same password manager note as the exchange password and that note syncs to a cloud you do not control.

The screen, and the first small transfer

A signature helps only if you read the device screen, not only the computer window. Malware can show one address and send another to the device. Compare the address and the amount on the device with what you meant, and cancel if a group of characters disagrees. A clipboard does not replace that look. An address shown only by the browser should be checked against the device when the model can show it.

Make the first transfer small and wait until it is visible on the network you selected. The same coin name often exists on several. A wrong network is not cured by open firmware or a secure element. If the test does not arrive, stop and read the maker's help. Install firmware from the official app and read what changed. A file from anywhere else is the same class of mistake as a pre-initialized marketplace device.

Who it fits

Ledger fits someone who wants a secure-element device, accepts firmware that is not fully open, and has decided whether a recovery service of the Recover kind is acceptable. Check the current product before you treat 2023 as the whole story. Trezor fits someone who wants firmware associated with open source and who will read the current model's spec instead of assuming it has no secure element. Neither fits someone who will photograph the words. The more careful device does not survive the more careless backup. A person who will not move coins off an exchange does not need either device yet. A person who will move them needs the phrase plan before the shopping tab.

This site did not open the chips, did not audit firmware, and did not buy a sample of devices to look for tampering. It did not time a transaction or test every coin. Support for a particular asset changes. Check the current asset list. A device that does not support the coin will not hold it, whatever the brand reputation. Prices were not copied. Supply-chain anecdotes are not a lab result. An affiliate link can pay a commission. It does not move your phrase out of a screenshot.

Limits

Firmware updates are part of owning the device. Read what an update says it changes. A screen that shows an address is only useful if you compare it with the address you meant. This article is not tax advice and not a view on whether to hold any coin. Plans for optional recovery products change. Check the current page of Ledger and of Trezor for the model, the backup story, and the price. Then decide whether you would enable a split backup at all. The inflation calculator will still be about purchasing power. The encryptor will still be a local note. Neither one is the phrase.

Questions people ask

What does a hardware wallet actually protect?

It holds a private key and signs a transaction on the device, so the key is not supposed to sit in the browser during ordinary use. The coins live on the network. The device is replaceable. The recovery phrase is not. Anyone with the phrase can recreate the keys and take the coins. A cloud note, a screenshot, and an email to yourself defeat both brands. Buy from the maker if you can. A pre-initialized device from a marketplace may already have a phrase someone else kept.

Should I turn on something like Ledger Recover?

In 2023 Ledger discussed Recover, an optional paid design to split a backup so a service could help restore a key after identity checks. It was widely criticised because it showed a path for key material to leave the device, even though it was optional. Decide whether you would enable anything like that before you buy. Then check what the product does now. The feature may have been renamed or limited. A 2023 argument does not describe today's checkbox, and today's checkbox does not erase the argument.

Are all Trezor devices without a secure element?

Do not assume that. Trezor is associated with open-source firmware, which means the signing code can be read and compared by people who are not the vendor. Newer models may differ, and a current model may include a secure element. Read the spec of the model you are about to pay for, not a forum post about an older one. Open firmware and a secure element are not one axis with a winner at the end. Write down what your sheet actually says.

Can I type my seed into the password generator to check it?

No. Do not type a hardware-wallet phrase into any website, including this one. The device generates the seed. The password generator is for account passwords and for an extra passphrase you might create and then write down offline. The AES encryptor locks a local note and cannot recover it if the password is lost. Uploading an encrypted seed is a cloud copy of the only secret, which is the failure the device cannot see. Keep the phrase offline.

Does a hardware wallet protect me from inflation?

No. Keeping coins away from a custodian is a different problem from the currency buying less later. A coin nobody else can move can still purchase less. The inflation calculator shows a basket's later cost and the buying power of today's sum, using a rate you type. That rate is an assumption, not a forecast and not a reason to hold any asset.
