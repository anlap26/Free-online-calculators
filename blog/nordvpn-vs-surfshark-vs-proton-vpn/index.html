NordVPN, Surfshark, and Proton VPN for travel and remote work

A way to compare three VPN brands on documents you can read, for a trip or a remote job, without a speed ranking this site did not earn.

A virtual private network is a tunnel from your device to a server the VPN company runs. Sites you open see that server's address rather than the address a hotel, a café, or your home provider assigned. On a network you do not control, the local router cannot easily read the pages inside the tunnel. A remote worker can also present one chosen exit country to a tool that is fussy about location. The tunnel ends at the VPN company. That company can see that you connected, even when a no-logs clause limits what it writes down. You are moving trust from the café to the provider. The trade can be worth making on a trip. It does not give you a new identity.

Travellers and remote workers usually want a network that is a poor place to snoop, enough devices covered without a surprise, and a company they can describe from documents: where it is incorporated, what the latest transparency report counted, and which audit a stranger can download. This page will not rank speed. One evening on one network does not survive a new server or a congested hotel.

How to compare without a lab

You do not need a bench of machines. You need the current plan page, the current transparency report, and a note of what the tunnel is for. Write down the country you will sit in, the exit country you want, and how many devices must be online together. Then read, for each company, the about page that names the legal entity, the transparency report that counts legal requests, the audit with a scope and a date, and the pricing page that states device limits. If a document is missing, that absence is a fact. Do not fill it from a forum.

A magazine star, a coupon ranking, and a banner about a quick server are advertising. If a document and an advertisement disagree, believe the document and check its date. A limit remembered from last year is not a limit you can use this month.

Jurisdiction and the transparency report

Jurisdiction means which country's rules the company cannot ignore. Check where it is based, which entity would receive an order, and what the transparency report says. Groups move holding companies. Read the legal name on the terms. A useful report counts requests received, requests answered, and the period. A sentence about loving privacy is not a report.

Proton is a Swiss company and says so in its own materials. Swiss registration is not a shield. It tells you which legal tradition to go and read. It does not prove that a given server kept nothing. For NordVPN and Surfshark, do the same reading rather than repeating a country from memory. Ownership moves. If the two brands sit in one group, check whether the report is per brand or combined. A promise written for one brand does not automatically cover the other.

Audits

These providers publish audit reports from time to time. Read them. This page will not invent a score. An audit has a date, a target, and a list of things the tester did not look at. A report on one application build does not cover the website, the payment system, and the server fleet. A report from two years ago does not describe the build you install today. Look for the product name, the version, the date, and the out-of-scope section.

A passed report can still list issues fixed later or accepted as they stand. A letter grade from a review site is not part of the PDF. If you cannot find the report on the company's own site, the claim is unfinished. Prefer the report you understood.

NordVPN

NordVPN is a large commercial VPN for ordinary devices. People look it up because the apps are widely available and the plan page is built for someone who wants a tunnel without assembling one. Verify the dull specifics. Which legal entity sells to a customer in your country. How many devices can hold a session at once, because that number has changed over the years and belongs on the current page, not in memory. Which blocker or filter features are included, and which stay off until you enable them. Whether the less famous apps, for a television or a router, fall under the audit you read.

Install before the flight and confirm that the address a site sees is the exit you chose. A remote worker also has the employer's rules to respect. A tunnel that breaks company policy is not a productivity tool, and calls still depend on the network under the tunnel.

Surfshark

Surfshark has marketed unlimited devices. That claim is why households and people with a stack of gadgets shortlist it. Confirm the wording on the current plan. Unlimited may mean every device you own, or it may sit beside a fair-use line, a cap on simultaneous sessions, or a rule about routers. Read whether a phone and a laptop online together count as two, and whether a router counts as one device or as a doorway for a whole flat.

Then read jurisdiction and the latest audit as you would for any brand. A lower advertised price is not a property of the tunnel. Discounts expire, and a long plan paid upfront differs from a monthly plan. Confirm the device story, then still read who operates the service.

Proton VPN

Proton VPN comes from Proton, the Swiss company many people already know for mail. Proton has published open-source VPN apps. You can read the clients instead of trusting the interface alone. Open source is not a full security proof. It means the code can be inspected and a claim can be compared with a file. It does not mean every server is open, or that every optional feature is visible. Read which parts are published and which build you are installing.

A free tier has existed. Confirm its limits on the current page. Those caps move, and they often mean fewer servers or a tighter device or speed cap. A free plan is a fair way to learn the apps and to run a leak check. It is a poor assumption for a month of client calls until you have read what it withholds. If you already use Proton mail, one account is convenient, and it is also one company holding the mailbox and the tunnel.

What a VPN does not do

A VPN does not anonymise you against an account you log into. The bank, the employer, the mail host, and the shop still see the account, and they may see the VPN address, which can trip their own checks. A VPN does not fix a reused password. If one secret opens mail and a shop, a leaked database opens both, tunnel or not. Build a unique secret in the password generator, and use the password-manager guide if you need a place to keep it. A VPN does not strip a photo. The EXIF remover drops embedded location on your device, before you upload.

A VPN does not put your colleagues on your clock. The meeting planner shows who is inside ordinary hours. The tunnel answers a different question. A VPN also does not make a restricted act lawful, and it does not override an employer or a border officer. This page compares three commercial tunnels. It is not advice about breaking a rule.

WebRTC, in plain language

Browsers can open a direct connection for calls. To find a path, the browser gathers candidate addresses, including ones on the local network and sometimes the public address your provider assigned. A page may read that list. The ordinary request can show the VPN server while the candidate list still shows the provider's address. That gap is a WebRTC leak: an extra address beside the tunnel, not a camera watching you.

The IP and WebRTC test asks public lookup services which address they see, then compares it with addresses from a WebRTC connection. A different public address in that list is the leak the page can show. A normal page cannot see your DNS resolver, and the test does not pretend to. Run it once with the VPN off, so you recognise your address, and once with it on. If the on-test still shows the off-test address, the tunnel is not covering what the page can see. Check the app's current setting for this behaviour. One café does not produce a ranking.

Who it fits

NordVPN fits a traveller who wants a mainstream paid app, has checked today's device limit, and has kept the transparency report. It is a weak fit if you need client source code. Surfshark fits a household with many devices, after the current unlimited-device wording is confirmed, including routers and simultaneous sessions. It is a weak fit when the only reason is a discount. Proton VPN fits someone who wants published client source, who wants to read about a Swiss company, or who has checked that the free tier matches the threat. It also fits an existing Proton user who accepts one company holding mail and the tunnel.

What this site did not measure

This site did not run a speed lab, did not buy a year of each brand, and did not count servers. It did not test a kill switch on every system, did not watch a connection across a flight, and did not interpret a statute for you. A larger server list on a marketing page is not automatically a better hotel exit. Uptime, support speed, and whether a streaming site works this week were not measured, and would go stale if they had been. Affiliate links can pay the publisher a commission. They do not change the checks. If a company moves offices or publishes a new report, prefer the new document over this page.

Limits

Prices, device caps, free-tier limits, and server lists change. Check the current page before you pay or before you tell a colleague that every gadget in the flat can connect. A network can block a VPN app, and a protocol that worked last year can fail on a hotel that interferes with it. Read the app's current help, not an old thread. This comparison is not a ranking and not a claim that any brand is the fastest. It is a way to read three products without pretending this site ran a laboratory.

Questions people ask

Does a VPN make me anonymous?

It hides the address a normal website sees by sending traffic through the provider's server. An account you log into still sees the account. Your bank, your employer, and your mail host can also see that the address belongs to a VPN, and their own checks may react to that. A reused password is a separate failure the tunnel does not repair. The provider itself can see that you connected. Read the transparency report for what they say they keep.

What is a WebRTC leak?

Browsers gather candidate addresses when they set up a direct connection, for example for a call. A page can read that list. Your ordinary requests may show the VPN server while the list still contains the address your internet provider assigned. That gap is the leak. It is not a camera watching you. The IP test on this site compares the public address a lookup sees with the WebRTC list. Run it with the VPN off and on, then read the app's current setting if the address did not change.

Which of the three is fastest?

This page does not know, and it will not award a speed title. A test on one evening, on one network, measures that evening. A hotel, a new server, and a congested hour change the result. Compare jurisdiction, the latest audit you can actually download, and the device limit on the plan you would pay for. If speed is the worry, measure it yourself on the network you use, with the app you will really install, and keep the result as your result rather than a ranking.

Does Surfshark include unlimited devices?

Surfshark has marketed unlimited devices. Confirm the sentence on the current plan before you rely on it. The wording can sit beside a fair-use line, a cap on simultaneous sessions, or a separate rule for routers. A phone and a laptop online together may count as two connections even when the marketing says unlimited. Write down the sentence you are buying. A discount does not answer the question, and a memory of last year's plan does not either.

Does Proton VPN still have a free tier?

A free tier has existed. The limits move, and they often mean fewer servers or a tighter cap on speed or devices. Confirm the current page before you plan a month of work calls around it. A free plan is a reasonable way to learn the apps and to run the leak test on your own network. Paid plans are what you compare with NordVPN and Surfshark. Published app source does not describe the free tier's limits.
