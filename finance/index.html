Finance calculators

Calculators for a mortgage or loan payment, simple and compound interest, a position you size from a stop, and the gap between a bank rate and the interbank rate.

These pages answer planning questions with the formula written out beside the form. Compound interest takes a nominal annual rate and an optional monthly contribution. The average-price tool turns several buys, including fees, into one break-even. Staking compares effective yearly rates. Trading fees add maker or taker commission and slippage, then solve the sell quote that gets you back to flat. FIRE turns yearly spending and a withdrawal rate into a portfolio number and, if you also type a contribution and a return, a rough time to reach it.

Inflation is kept separate on purpose. A balance can grow while the currency buys less, and mixing those two rates inside one number hides the assumption. The currency page compares the amount a bank delivers with the amount the interbank rate would have delivered. Position size converts a percent of the account and a stop distance into a quantity. None of this is a forecast. A rate you type is an assumption, and tax sits outside every formula here.

The guides next to these tools discuss a broker used by people who invest across countries, two large crypto exchanges, and two hardware wallets. They describe what to check. They do not quote a fee schedule that the firm can change next week. Use the calculator with the numbers from the schedule you were actually offered.

Questions people ask

Which finance calculator should I open first?

If the question is a future balance, start with compound interest. If the question is a coin you already bought at several prices, start with average price. If the question is how much you can lose on one trade, start with position size.

Do these pages recommend a broker or an exchange?

No. The guides describe what to check and do not link to a broker or an exchange. The currency page links to labelled travel partners. The arithmetic does not depend on a link, and a residency rule can block a product a guide mentions.

Are the default rates a prediction?

No. Defaults are there so the form is not empty. Replace them with the rate you want to test.
