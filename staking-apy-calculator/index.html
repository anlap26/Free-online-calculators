Staking APY calculator

Type an APY or an APR for each coin, see the effective yearly rate, and sort the rows by gain rather than by the headline rate.

Read the yield where it is offered

This page compares rates you type. It does not need a Bybit or Binance account. If you open one, read the current yield, the lockup, and the fee there, because posted yields change. The calculator does not lock a rate in, and it runs without an account.

Open BybitOpen Binance

Each row is one coin, with an amount and a rate you type. The years apply to every row. If the rate is an APY, the future value is the amount times (1 plus APY) to the power of the years. The effective yearly rate is the APY itself. The compounds box is ignored in that mode, because an APY already includes compounding. Do not compound it again in your head, and do not also divide it by 12 and run a monthly loop. That second pass counts the same growth twice and the pile looks larger than the definition allows. A posted yield is not a promise that the rate will still be there next month. Yields change. This page only multiplies the rate you typed.

APR is a different kind of input

If the rate is an APR compounded n times a year, the effective yearly rate is (1 plus APR divided by n) to the power of n, minus 1. The future value then uses that effective rate once a year: amount times (1 plus effective) to the power of the years. It does not compound monthly on top. Take 12 percent APR compounded 12 times. The effective rate is 1.01 to the 12th, minus 1, about 12.68 percent. On 1000 for one year the future value is about 1126.83. If you then treat 12.68 percent as an APY and also picture monthly compounding, you have counted the year twice. For a one-year APR row, the future value is simply the amount times one plus the effective rate.

Sort by gain, not by the headline

Rows are ordered by gain, largest first. Gain is the future value minus the amount you typed. A large balance at a modest rate can outrank a small balance at a high rate. Put 1000 at 5 percent APY for 2 years next to 5000 at 2 percent APY for the same 2 years. The first becomes 1102.50, a gain of 102.50. The second becomes 5202, a gain of 202, because 1.02 squared is 1.0404. The lower rate ranks first. The combined value and the combined gain add the rows. They are not a blended APY, and they do not describe a mix that you rebalance.

What the yield figure leaves out

  • The rate is constant. A banner rate from this week is not locked in by the formula.
  • Some products lock the coins until a timer ends. The formula has no countdown.
  • Some proof-of-stake assets can be reduced by slashing if a validator breaks a rule. The number here does not haircut the amount.
  • A stablecoin yield is not a bank deposit and it is not a government-insured balance.
  • Inflation eats the nominal yield. A gain in coin terms can still buy less.

The inflation calculator is where a currency's inflation rate sits next to a nominal return, so you can see what is left after prices move. Taxes and trading fees are outside this multiplication. The trading fee calculator is the place for a percent taken on a fill. Bybit and Binance show yields that change, along with lockups and fees that also change. Opening an account lets you read the current terms. It does not paste that rate into this page, and the page works if you never sign up. The Binance and Bybit guide says what to check, including the fact that coins on an exchange are not in your own wallet. This comparison is arithmetic on the rates you chose to test.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between APY and APR for staking?

APY is already the effective yearly rate. The future value is the amount times (1 plus APY) to the power of the years, and you should not compound that figure again. APR is a nominal rate. The page turns it into an effective yearly rate with (1 plus APR divided by n) to the power of n, minus 1, and only then applies that rate once a year. The compounds field matters for APR and is ignored for APY.

How much is 1000 at 5 percent APY after 2 years?

It is 1102.50. The first year grows 1000 to 1050, and the second year grows 1050 by 5 percent, which adds 52.50. That second step is the compounding already inside the APY. Do not also split 5 percent into months and compound those months on top. The gain is 102.50 under a rate that stays at 5 percent for both years. If the posted yield changes, this figure no longer describes the product.

Why does the table sort by gain instead of by the rate?

Gain is future value minus the amount you typed, and a larger pile at a lower rate can make more money than a small pile at a high rate. 1000 at 5 percent APY for 2 years gains 102.50. 5000 at 2 percent APY for 2 years gains 202. The lower rate ranks first. The combined gain adds the rows. It is not a blended APY of a portfolio, and it does not assume you move coins between rows.

Is a stablecoin yield the same as a bank deposit?

No. A stablecoin reward is a rate offered by a protocol or an exchange. It is not a bank deposit, and it is not insured the way a deposit scheme insures cash. The coin can lose its peg, the rate can change, and a lockup can stop you selling. Slashing can reduce some proof-of-stake assets when a validator breaks a rule. None of those events is subtracted in this formula. The number is the rate you typed, held constant.

Does staking yield survive inflation?

The formula does not know. It grows the amount by the rate you typed and stops there. Inflation can eat that nominal gain, so the same coins buy less of ordinary goods even when the row shows a profit. Put the currency inflation next to a nominal return on the inflation calculator if you want the gap made explicit. Yields also change, and a lockup can keep you in the product after the banner rate has moved.
